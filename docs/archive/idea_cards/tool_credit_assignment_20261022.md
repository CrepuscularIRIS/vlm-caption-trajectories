# Decision-State Baselines for Follow-up Tool Calls: The Four Before/After Cases as Credit Assignment at an Unchanged RLOO Objective

> Credit for a follow-up crop can come from the acting policy's own answer-now value at that decision, used as a baseline on every token of the decision whatever is sampled there, so nothing is added to the reward and RLOO's expected gradient is kept. Close neighbours consume the same answer-now information as a price, a gate or an added reward, which changes the objective. We predict that this baseline raises recovery after a misleading first crop (E2) at the RLOO crop count, that a within-item shuffle of the same values does not, and that the same values placed only on crop turns shift the crop count. Together these measure how much of four-case tool credit is credit assignment and how much is a hidden change of objective.

## Abstract
In multi-turn crop-and-zoom reinforcement learning (RL) for vision-language models with final-answer reward, every generated turn receives the same outcome advantage (`2505.14362#s1`, `2602.01334#s3`). A follow-up tool call's credit should depend on whether it moved the acting policy's own answer from wrong to right, kept it right, kept it wrong, or moved it from right to wrong, but the answer state just before the call is never observed. Available judges of that state are either a different reader (`2512.16918#s1`, `2602.14201#s2`) or a reachability score consumed as reward (`2606.11209#s1`). We propose to estimate the policy's own answer-now value b(s_k) at each follow-up decision state with commit-prefilled Monte-Carlo forks and to subtract it as a baseline from every generated token of that decision, whatever action is sampled, so that the four before/after cases become the sign table of r_i − b(s_k) while RLOO's expected gradient is kept under stated assumptions; placed only on crop turns, the same value changes the objective, so the pair separates credit from objective change. If b(s_k) is load-bearing, E2 accuracy (recovery after an offset first crop) at step 160 increases relative to the r1 RLOO run and relative to a within-item same-index shuffle of b, with E1 not lower and crops per question inside r1's interval, while the crop-only variant moves crops per question. We expect a small E2 increase; a null across DB, PERM and r1 would support the reading that the objective is the bottleneck.

## In plain words
Vision-language models that answer questions about images can be trained, with reinforcement learning (RL), to crop and zoom into the picture several times before answering. They are rewarded only for the final answer, so every step of an attempt gets the same credit, even a zoom that fixed a wrong answer or one that misled a right one. Earlier work judged a zoom by a different model's view or a separate no-tool pass, or turned such scores into extra reward, which changes what training optimises. The idea: at each follow-up zoom decision, make the model answer immediately a few times from that exact point, score those answers against the key, and subtract that 'answer-now' score from the final reward for every token of that decision, whether it zoomed or answered. Under stated conditions the average training direction is unchanged from the leave-one-out method (RLOO) it modifies; only who gets the credit changes. If this matters, recovery after a misleading first zoom improves, and shuffling the scores within a question removes the gain; putting the same scores only on zoom turns should instead change how often the model zooms. Main risk: the scores may not make credit less noisy on real runs, which an offline check tests before training.

## Motivation
**Why prior work stopped:** arxiv:2602.01334 — did: partitions crop-and-zoom outcomes into exactly the four before/after cases (tool-free correctness × call vs no-call × tool-available correctness) and reports Call Gain and Call Harm as Mass × Policy × Quality. Did not: partition per call inside a trajectory, or route any credit by the partition; the one reward variant tried (a +0.1 call bonus) moves call intensity only, and objectives for selective correction are left as future work. Structural reason: a missing measurement — the before state comes from a separate tool-free pass of the checkpoint at item level, which the authors call an operational proxy, so no per-call, in-trajectory before state exists to key credit on. arxiv:2512.16918 — did: sets the sign of an added tool reward from an offline Tool Benefit Score of a separate Qwen2.5-VL-72B (runs with vs without tools), decaying with call count. Did not: look at what each crop returned or whether the acting policy itself benefited; Appendix D concedes the single reference model may bias the estimate. Structural reason: a missing assumption-relaxation — it assumes a fixed per-item reference reader stands in for the policy's state, so every call on an item shares one benefit. arxiv:2602.14201 — did: scales the tool term by the base model's gold probability on the full image times a per-category step allowance. Did not: condition on the trajectory; removing the term barely changes the tool-call ratio (68.44% vs 67.47%), and its necessity judge has no image access. Structural reason: a missing measurement — the signal is item-level and exogenous, so it credits that a zoom happened, not what the zoom changed. arxiv:2608.12764 — did: weights steps inside the outcome advantage by a self-teacher log-likelihood ratio from a periodically re-synced policy copy. Did not: change the update direction or give correct trajectories any process signal; text-only 8B search agents, single runs. Structural reason: a missing mechanism — a multiplicative weight under the trajectory's sign cannot de-credit a redundant step in a correct trajectory or stop uniformly punishing a necessary failed step. arxiv:2606.11209 — did: values each step prefix by the current policy's own continuations scored against gold and averages the values into an added reward. Did not: isolate the step's contribution (it measures whether the answer is still reachable); reports noise and multi-rollout cost and gives no compute-matched outcome-only baseline. Structural reason: a missing mechanism — the value is consumed as reward, which changes the objective, and full continuations estimate reachability rather than the value of answering now. arxiv:2509.14480 — did: places judge-derived per-turn rewards at each turn's final token under PPO with GAE, which degraded training below vanilla PPO. Did not: test per-turn credit that adds no reward; its judge uses ground-truth tool-call annotations, which crop tasks lack. Structural reason: a missing tool — no annotation-free per-turn signal, and the negative result confounds per-turn credit with a per-turn change of the reward.

**Stakes:** This is stated at the class level of terminal-reward multi-turn tool RL in which a call's value depends on the policy's pre-call state, as phase1 frames it, and instantiated on the Qwen3-VL-4B crop policy. Who pays (G1): practitioners training crop-tool policies, because right-but-redundant crops keep full positive credit; in the primary instance crops per question rise from 1.01 after SFT to 1.93-2.08 under RLOO with λ 0 or 0.01 (host measurement, phase1 G1), and misleading right-to-wrong crops share blame with answer tokens. Who pays (G2): anyone keying credit to an exogenous reader, since a reader right about .53 vs the policy's .66 relabels right-before calls as wrong-before. Who pays (G3): the mentor's requested objective change may be ruled out on one untested instability result (`2509.14480#s1`). What becomes available if the gap closes: a per-follow-up-call credit keyed to the acting policy's own state at RLOO's expected objective, and with it a measured answer to whether four-case tool credit acts as credit assignment or as a hidden objective change — the question the mentor's 10-21 note poses about which tokens receive the outcome.

## Core claim
The acting policy's own Monte-Carlo answer-now value at each follow-up tool decision, subtracted as an action-independent baseline from every token of that decision, gives each follow-up call credit keyed to the four before/after cases while keeping RLOO's expected gradient (under assumptions a-d), so that it and its crop-turn-only counterpart separate credit assignment from objective change.

- G1: for every follow-up decision state s_k with k ≥ 2, c_ik = r_i − b(s_k) with b the commit-prefilled fork value makes a follow-up call's credit depend on the sampled branch's return relative to answering now; read against the final outcome its sign pattern is wrong-to-right ≈ +1, right-to-right ≈ (1 − λk') − (1 − λ(k−1))·mean_j z_kj, wrong-to-wrong = −b(s_k), right-to-wrong ≈ −1 (conditional on b approximating Q(s_k, commit); the first call keeps RLOO's A_i by design and is not routed).
- G1 (construction): under (a) fork randomness independent of turn-k and later sampling, (b) stop-gradient b, (c) the same b on every token of turn k whatever action is sampled, and (d) on-policy sampling with an action-independent normaliser, DB and its within-item shuffle PERM have RLOO's expected gradient, while the crop-turn-only placement adds −π(1−π)(V_stop − J) (established in coherence's executed toy; approximate under PPO clipping, staleness and token-mean by arm-specific amounts).
- G3: because DB adds no reward, comparing training stability (entropy, length, direct-answer share, collapse) of DB against r1 and an outcome-gated routing arm separates 'per-turn credit destabilises' from 'per-turn reward changes the objective'.
- Bottleneck part (b), endogenous judges: the matched pair DB vs crop-only, at identical forks and b, measures how much of four-case routing's effect on crops per question is objective change rather than credit (hypothesis; tested in `falsification`).

## Method
1. S1: Audit train/test overlap (E16) — Input: library A's 4,040 items, the r1 RL prompt pool and E1's 552 items (MME-RealWorld 339, MMMU-Pro 39, V* 112, WorldBench 62), as images plus question text. Operation: perceptual image hashing (pHash) with Hamming distance ≤ hash_tau (default 4; raised to 8 if a manual check of 50 sampled matches finds misses), plus normalised question-text exact match, plus a check of the source-group split recorded at library construction (implementation on disk: `.forge/studies/rl-objective-credit/prep_db/overlap_audit.py`). Output: an overlap list (item id, benchmark, match type) and per-benchmark counts; S11 consumes it to report every E1/E2 number on all items and on the overlap-free subset. CPU only.
2. S2: Unit-test the advantage function — Input: the toy item of `core_mechanism` (shared s_1; crop to a good view with V_stop .9 or a bad view with V_stop .2; a useless second crop, so TRUE = 0 at s_2), π(crop) = .9, G = 16, m_now = 2, 40,000 groups, fed as synthetic batches to the production advantage function of S5 (`prep_db/db_credit.py` `apply_db`, tests in `test_db_credit.py`). Operation: Monte-Carlo estimate of the per-state mean score-function gradient for RLOO, DB, PERM and crop-only, plus a token-mean variant with crop turns twice as long as answer turns. Output: a per-state table of mean gradient and MC standard error per arm. Pass rule: DB and PERM within 3 MC standard errors of the analytic TRUE gradient at every state, and the crop-only bias within 3 standard errors of −π(1−π)(V_stop − J); PERM must draw from the same item and the same index (`test_perm_draws_same_item_same_index`). Failure blocks all GPU use.
3. S3: Run the offline fork pass and gate (no training) — Input: r1's step-40 and step-160 checkpoints and r1's prompt pool; for the second size, the same pass runs on the RLOO-2B step-40 checkpoint (see S11). The r1 training-rollout logger stores only counts, correctness and scores per rollout, so unless a full token ledger exists, regenerate G = 16 rollouts per prompt from each checkpoint with r1's sampling settings and log the per-turn ledger (token ids, crop boxes, returned views). Operation: rebuild every decision state s_k with k ≥ 2 (natural answer turns included) and draw m ∈ {1, 2, 4} answer-now forks per state with vLLM prefix caching (prefill '<think>Step k | commit', same policy, rollout temperature, cap max_fork_tokens; implementation on disk: `prep_db/offline_forks.py`). Output: (i) b(s_k) per state; (ii) E[c²] on k ≥ 2 turns with item-bootstrap 95% CIs for DB, PERM/KLOO, crop-only and RLOO; (ii-b) the crop-only shaping sign σ from WORD-position logits at s_k (one forward pass on the cached prefix) and J_q = the item's mean return; (iii) lever mass: share of k ≥ 2 turns, own-state four-case shares by k, share of decisive follow-up turns in uniform groups; (iv) prefill check at natural answer states (forced-commit fork accuracy and letters vs the natural answer) [author decision: what disagreement level between forced-commit and natural answers, if any, blocks training, since biased b adds only variance]; (v) optional frozen-reader four-case cross-tab against own-state labels on the existing reader server. Selection rules: m_now = the smallest m whose E[c²] is within one bootstrap SE of the m = 4 value; max_fork_tokens = the 99th percentile of natural answer-turn length, set to TURN_MAX_TOKENS = 640 if more than trunc_tau = 1% of forks truncate. Gate: S4-S10 run only if the 95% CI of the DB/RLOO E[c²] ratio on k ≥ 2 turns lies below 1; otherwise record not_testable (per size).
4. S4: Generate rollouts with forks during training — Input: each batch's completed multi-turn rollouts from the EasyR1 crop environment (`pipeline/grpo/v51_credit/easyr1_caption.generate_sequences_multiturn`), with the per-round token ledger (ids, images, grid) at every s_k. Operation: for every decision state s_k with k ≥ 2, whatever action the policy then sampled, enqueue m_now commit-prefilled forks (from S3) to the same vLLM engine with prefix caching enabled through the engine overrides; fork seeds come from a separate counter that never advances the main-rollout seed counter, so main sampling keeps r1's seed stream; an unparsable or truncated fork scores z = 0, the main-rollout rule; if the context limit leaves no room at s_k there is no turn k to credit and the fork is skipped. Output: z_kj ∈ {0,1}, b(s_k) per (rollout, turn) carried with the batch (as `caption_cases` already travel in multi_modal_data) to S5; plus fork parse rate, truncation rate and fork token count for S10. The reader URL/model environment requirement must be relaxed for DB, PERM and crop-only, which do not use the reader.
5. S5: Build the token advantage tensor — Input: r_i, the unchanged RLOO A_i, turn spans from the existing turn segmentation (`pipeline/grpo/v51_credit`, tool_credit.segments over response_mask) and b(s_k) from S4. Operation: turn-1 generated tokens get A_i; every generated token of turn k ≥ 2 (from 'Step k' through WORD, captions and bbox or answer letter) gets r_i − b(s_k); observation tokens stay masked; installed as a compute_advantage patch modelled on tool_credit.install_advantage_patch, selected by CAPTION_DB_MODE ∈ {db, perm, croponly, rloo}. Output: a token-level advantage tensor of the response's shape, consumed by S6; the same function backs S2.
6. S6: Apply the policy update — Input: S5's token advantages. Operation: EasyR1 PPO-clip with r1's settings (lr 1e-6, no KL, no dynamic sampling, 12 prompts × 16 rollouts, 165 steps, token-mean aggregation, r1's seed and clip range, checkpoints every 40 steps), λ = 0.01 fixed by the mentor. Output: checkpoints consumed by S11. Through the follow-up turns' WORD, caption and bbox tokens the update changes whether and where the policy looks again after a view, and hence its final answers.
7. S7: Run the PERM negative control (candidate step 7) — Input: as S4-S6, plus each item's set of decision states grouped by index k. Operation: each k ≥ 2 state of rollout i receives the b of a state at the same index k from another rollout of the same item, drawn uniformly and independently of any action sampled at the state, applied to all tokens of turn k; fallback KLOO when no other rollout reached index k: the leave-one-out mean return of the item's other rollouts that reached k, else the item's leave-one-out mean return. Implementation: CAPTION_DB_MODE=perm (`perm_baselines` in db_credit.py), verified to pool by (item, index), not batch-wide, before launch. Output: PERM token advantages to S6; keeps fork compute, item and index information and RLOO's expected gradient, and breaks only the state-to-own-value link (the matched-concentration shuffle of arxiv:2609.02417).
8. S8: Run the crop-only contrast arm (candidate step 7b) — Input: the same forks and b(s_k) as DB, plus the parsed WORD of each turn. Operation: r_i − b(s_k) only on k ≥ 2 turns whose sampled WORD ∈ {locate, re_examine, fallback}; commit and answer turns at k ≥ 2 and turn 1 keep A_i. This is action-dependent at the call-or-answer token, so it adds the shaping term −π(1−π)(V_stop − J), reproduced in the S2 toy (−.0142/+.0132). Implementation: CAPTION_DB_MODE=croponly in db_credit.py; at card time the mode and a toy test are present on disk (the revise notes still describe it as not yet implemented), and the closed-form check of S2 must pass before launch. Output: crop-only token advantages to S6; DB vs crop-only holds information fixed and changes only action-dependence.
9. S9: Run the OGAR-style contemporary arm (candidate step 8) — Input: rollouts with at least one crop. Operation: read the policy's greedy commit-prefilled answer at s_1 (a1) and after the final crop (a2), Δ = correct(a2) − correct(a1); route: Δ > 0, all tokens A_i; Δ = 0 with a1 correct, tool-turn tokens min(A_i, 0); Δ = 0 with a1 wrong, tool-turn tokens max(A_i, 0); Δ < 0, tool-turn tokens keep A_i and answer-turn tokens max(A_i, 0); direct-answer rollouts keep A_i; the gated Δ reward channel is omitted and this is not the TACO authors' code. Output: OGAR-style token advantages to S6; reported against r1's intervals (E01), not used for attribution.
10. S10: Log monitors (E06, E15, G3) — Input: per-step training state of each arm. Operation: logging, no estimator. Output per step: entropy, response length, direct-answer share, crops/q by k, E[c²] on k ≥ 2 turns, fork parse and truncation rates, wall-clock overhead relative to r1; every 40 steps, mean b(s_2) against held-out accuracy on 192 validation items. Consumed by the stability comparison (G3) and the overhead report.
11. S11: Evaluate — Input: checkpoints at 80 and 160 (40/120 in the full campaign) of DB, crop-only, PERM and OGAR-style, the existing r1 checkpoints (GRPO-A and r0 for reference), and S1's overlap list. Operation: E1 (552 items × 4 samples, T = 0.7) and E2 (first crop replaced by an offset box) on all items and on the overlap-free subset; paired item-bootstrap 95% CIs with 2,000 resamples; follow-up call gain and harm by answer-now forks on E1 rollouts. Output: accuracy and paired differences, crops/q by k, tokens and latency per query, P(own-wrong → right | follow-up call), P(own-right → wrong | follow-up call), and the share of E2 trajectories that re-crop and end correct. Second size: Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct with library-A SFT on the 4B recipe (LoRA r16, 2 epochs), RLOO-2B on r1's recipe, its own S3 gate on the RLOO-2B step-40 checkpoint, DB-2B only if that gate passes, both evaluated at 80 and 160 the same way; if the 2B SFT fails the caption protocol (parse rate) [author decision: the parse-rate threshold that counts as failing] or its gate fails, the 2B pair is recorded not_testable and the 4B claim is unchanged.

## Key equations
- `S2: r_i = correct_i · (1 − λ·N_i); A_i = r_i − (1/(G−1)) · Σ_{j≠i} r_j — the mentor's RLOO reward with crop count N_i and the leave-one-out advantage (λ = 0.01, G = 16).`
- `S2: b(s_k) = (1 − λ·(k−1)) · (1/m_now) · Σ_{j=1..m_now} z_kj, with z_kj = 1[parsed letter of commit-prefilled fork j at s_k = gold] — the stop-gradient Monte-Carlo estimate of Q(s_k, commit), the policy's expected reward if it answered now after k−1 crops.`
- `S2: c_ik = r_i − b(s_k) for k ≥ 2 — the decision-state credit of rollout i at follow-up decision k.`
- `S2: bias_croponly(s) = −π(crop|s) · (1 − π(crop|s)) · (V_stop(s) − J) per visit of s, J = the item's expected return — the expected-gradient term the crop-turn-only placement adds to the call logit.`
- `S3: m_now = min{ m ∈ {1,2,4} : E_m[c²] ≤ E_4[c²] + SE_boot(E_4[c²]) } — the fork-count selection rule.`
- `S3: R = E_DB[c²] / E_RLOO[A²] over k ≥ 2 turns; train iff the upper end of the item-bootstrap 95% CI of R is < 1 — the gate.`
- `S3: σ = mean_{k≥2 states} π(crop|s_k) · (1 − π(crop|s_k)) · (b(s_k) − J_q) — the measured shaping sign that sets crop-only's predicted crops/q direction.`
- `S5: adv_t = A_i if t ∈ turn 1; adv_t = r_i − b(s_k) if t is a generated token of turn k ≥ 2; t masked if an observation token — the DB token advantage.`
- `S5: E[ ∇_θ log π_θ(y_t | y_<t) · b(s_k) ] = 0 for every generated token t of turn k, given (a) fork randomness independent of turn-k and later sampling, (b) stop-gradient b, (c) the same b(s_k) on every token of turn k whatever is sampled, (d) on-policy sampling with an action-independent normaliser — why DB keeps RLOO's expected gradient (approximate under PPO clipping, staleness and token-mean).`
- `S7: b_PERM(s_k^i) = b(s_k^j), j ~ Uniform{ j ≠ i in item q with a state at index k }; else KLOO = mean{ r_j : j ≠ i, j reached k }; else (1/(G−1)) · Σ_{j≠i} r_j — the within-item same-index shuffle.`
- `S8: adv_t = r_i − b(s_k) if t ∈ turn k ≥ 2 and WORD_k ∈ {locate, re_examine, fallback}; else A_i — the crop-only placement.`
- `S9: Δ = correct(a2) − correct(a1); tool-turn adv = A_i (Δ > 0), min(A_i, 0) (Δ = 0, a1 correct), max(A_i, 0) (Δ = 0, a1 wrong), A_i with answer-turn adv max(A_i, 0) (Δ < 0) — OGAR-style routing.`
- `S11: gain = P(own-wrong → right | follow-up call), harm = P(own-right → wrong | follow-up call), with own state = fork answer at s_k — the fork-measured follow-up call gain and harm.`

## Closest prior work
- arxiv:2609.22910: CounterCredit already computes the policy's own answer-now value at every visual call's realized prefix, multi-call included. The missing step is to consume that value as an action-independent baseline on every token of the decision, instead of as a gate and price, which keeps RLOO's expected gradient and lets one arm pair separate credit assignment from objective change.
- arxiv:2602.01334: They partition items into the four before/after cases using a separate tool-free pass of the same checkpoint, and keep the partition descriptive (Mass x Policy x Quality), leaving objectives for selective correction to future work. We partition every follow-up decision inside a trajectory by the acting policy's own answer-now value at that exact state, which includes its earlier crops. We then turn the partition into token credit that keeps RLOO's expected gradient, by placing the value on every turn of the decision, answer turns included. We also measure how much of a four-case rule's effect is an objective change (the OGAR-style contrast).
- arxiv:2512.16918: They set the sign of an added tool reward per item from an offline Qwen2.5-VL-72B tool benefit score, decaying with call count, so the objective changes and every call on an item shares one benefit. We add no reward. The counterfactual is the acting policy's own answer-now value, recomputed online at each follow-up decision state, so two follow-up calls of the same item get different credit according to what each one's branch returned.
- arxiv:2602.14201: They scale an efficiency term by the base model's gold probability on the full image times a per-category step allowance (item-level, exogenous), and their necessity judge has no image access. We use no base model, categories or allowance. The before state is the policy's own answer from the exact prefix of each follow-up call, and it enters only as a baseline.
- arxiv:2608.12764: They multiply the GRPO advantage of incorrect trajectories by self-teacher step weights. These keep the outcome sign and give correct trajectories no process signal, so a redundant step in a correct trajectory keeps full credit. Our follow-up credit is set by subtraction in correct and incorrect trajectories alike and in uniform groups. It is near 0 for redundant (or exactly minus the crop cost under lambda) and failed calls, and about +-1 for decisive ones. It needs no privileged context or teacher re-sync, and its expected gradient equals RLOO's under the stated assumptions.
- arxiv:2606.11209: They value each step prefix by free continuations of the current policy scored against gold (reachability) and average the values into an added trajectory reward. We fork only the stop branch, so r - b measures the sampled action's return relative to answering now, not reachability. It is used as a baseline on that decision's tokens rather than as reward, and each fork is a single short answer turn rather than a full continuation.
- arxiv:2509.14480: They place GPT-4.1 per-turn scores, conditioned on ground-truth tool-call annotations, at turn ends, and this degraded PPO. We use no judge and no annotations, which crop tasks lack. Our per-turn credit adds no reward and keeps RLOO's expected gradient, which separates 'per-turn credit destabilises' from 'per-turn reward changes the objective' (G3 monitors).
- arxiv:2609.22910: CounterCredit computes, at every visual call's realized prefix (multi-call included), the policy's own answer-now score U_now and the decision value D = U_real - U_now. It consumes D through a verification gate q = min(E, D) and a bounded cashback/rent price added to the reward, with a dual-channel advantage. The objective therefore changes, and no arm separates credit assignment from that change. We use the same answer-now information only as an action-independent baseline on every token of the decision, add no reward, and keep RLOO's expected gradient. DB vs crop-only then measures the credit-vs-objective split that its 6.3-9.4 point gain leaves open.
- arxiv:2606.30251: TACO reads greedy prefilled probes before and after the tool. OGAR applies a 0/1 mask to the outcome advantage A1 by regime ('removes, never reverses'), plus a gated Delta reward channel. In multi-call branches the post-probe is read after the final call, at branch level, and per-call attribution is stated future work. We assign credit per follow-up decision inside multi-call trajectories, with an MC stop value used as a baseline rather than a mask or reward. An OGAR-style re-implementation is trained as the contemporary arm.
- arxiv:2410.01679: VinePPO re-feeds intermediate states to the current policy, samples K full continuations, takes V_MC(s_t) as their mean return, and uses the TD advantage r + gamma V_MC(s_{t+1}) - V_MC(s_t) on all tokens of a step. That is the same fork-baseline operation. We restrict the fork to the stop branch, so b = Q(s_k, commit) rather than V^pi(s_k), and use return minus baseline rather than a TD difference. Credit on a call turn then estimates Q(call) - Q(commit), which is what yields the four before/after cases as a sign table. With V^pi those cases do not appear. The unbiasedness property is shared and conceded.
- arxiv:2605.27788: CARL trains a critic on MC targets at tool-call boundaries and uses segment advantages V(s_{k+1}) - V(s_k), with R - V(s_N) for the last segment, on text-only Qwen2.5 tool use. We train no critic. b is a per-batch fork estimate of the stop-branch value at each visual follow-up decision, used as a baseline on that decision's tokens.
- arxiv:2510.14967: IGPO adds a per-turn reward equal to the marginal gain in log-probability of the gold answer, z-normalised and accumulated as a discounted return, in multi-turn text search. That is reward use, so the objective changes. We add no turn reward. The answer-now value enters only as a baseline, and the expected gradient stays RLOO's.

## Feasibility
artifacts: Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (open weights, full fine-tuning access), library A (existing, self-built), the existing r1 checkpoints and prompt pool, EasyR1 and vLLM (both named in `baselines.json`), E1 built from MME-RealWorld, MMMU-Pro, V* and WorldBench, and E2 as a host perturbation of E1; no closed resource is needed — feasible. theory: no theoretical contribution is claimed; the unbiasedness of a state-dependent baseline is conceded as textbook, and the construction is unit-tested (S2) and was executed by coherence on a 3-level toy — n/a. engineering: the binding cost is S4, enqueuing commit-prefilled forks inside the multi-turn rollout loop from the per-round ledger with prefix caching and a separate seed counter, and carrying b per turn to the advantage patch; S3 additionally needs rollout regeneration because the r1 logger stored no turns; db_credit.py, offline_forks.py, overlap_audit.py and test_db_credit.py exist on disk; open author decisions: the S3(iv) prefill-disagreement level that blocks training, and the S11 2B parse-rate threshold — tight. compute: `resources.estimate` gives about 60 GPU-h for gate plus wave 1 (the first decisive test) and about 145 GPU-h for the probe; the authorized limit fixes 2 GPUs per arm, single seed, probe stage and states no GPU-hour cap, and the plan respects the 2-per-arm form; training arms with forks dominate, the +30-50% fork overhead is unexecuted, it grows at m_now = 4 (about 8 forks per rollout vs about 4 at m_now = 2, derived from crops/q 2.08), and the S3 regeneration GPU-h may not be inside the 4 GPU-h offline figure — tight. falsification: all arms reuse the r1 recipe and existing evaluation sets; the intervention on `load_bearing_variable` (b(s_k) vs its within-item shuffle vs its crop-only placement) is a mode switch on one advantage function — feasible. overall: tight; the single binding constraint is the S3 gate — whether own-state baselines lower the second moment of follow-up credit on real r1 rollouts (coherence's toy gave a ratio of .943 and showed DB adds variance in all-correct groups), since without it there is no first-order route to a different policy at an unchanged expected gradient.

## Kill switch
If Any of: (1) the S3 gate fails — the 95% CI of the DB/RLOO E[c²] ratio on k ≥ 2 turns includes or exceeds 1 (recorded not_testable per size); (2) PERM matches DB on E2 accuracy at step 160 (the paired CI of DB − PERM includes 0), so the state-matched answer-now value is not load-bearing; (3) DB's E2 accuracy at step 160 does not increase over r1, or DB − r1 on E1 has a paired CI below 0; (4) a paper appears with the same mechanism: the acting policy's stop-branch answer-now value used as an action-independent baseline on every token of a tool decision at an unchanged outcome objective., then (1): stop before any training and record that own-state baselines do not reduce follow-up credit variance on this setting. (2) or (3): park the DB method; salvage the DB vs crop-only crops/q comparison and the DB/PERM/r1 null as the answer to the mentor's question (four-case routing acts through the objective, not credit), and record the lesson that at an unchanged expected gradient per-call credit did not change what 165 steps learn. (4): stop and record the scooping paper's delta, keeping only the credit-vs-objective measurement if it is not covered..
