# Make Intermediate Steps Explicit and Score Them
_id: `reward_explicit_intermediates`_

**Plain alias**. _Make intermediate steps explicit and score them_

**Definition**. Force the solution process to emit an explicit intermediate (evidence, subgoal, artifact, or a governed reasoning span) and attach a direct reward or check to it, instead of crediting only the final outcome.

**Operational signature**. identify the step that outcome-only credit misses → require it as an explicit output → define a check (fidelity, sufficiency, budget, enabling downstream success) → add it to the objective

**When to apply**. When correct final answers can be reached through wrong or ungrounded processes and outcome reward alone cannot tell them apart.

**Evidence base**. primary-tagged n=123, oral 3, spotlight 4, poster 81, reject 35; supporting-only n=47. HC not assigned in this run. The success side is thin (7 papers; the corpus has only 13 orals in total), so poster papers are used as supporting accepted evidence throughout and are marked as such.

## Success conditions (from Oral/Spotlight; Poster as support)
- **The check on the intermediate is computed by something the policy cannot trivially satisfy by wording: an independent proxy that sees only the intermediate, alignment to annotated or cross-verified structure, or a deterministic program.** — rationale: the spotlight scores perception by whether a separate text-only reasoner can solve the task from the written perception alone; one oral rewards alignment of a generated task graph to annotated nodes and edges; another oral keeps only reasoning hops that pass cross-model necessity and non-redundancy checks. Posters with deterministic checkers (an event index used as a symbolic verifier, executable assertions checked against a maintained state) receive the same praise: no judge to hallucinate and less room to hack. [ids: duzdftKtUA, 3eTr9dGwJv, JEGDp1E4OH; poster support: LuzGvybw2S, pxN9ZXdBhV]
- **The intermediate's form follows a structure taken from the task or from a human expert procedure, not a generic "think" span.** — rationale: the accepted top-tier papers take the intermediate's form from an expert workflow (a forensic quick-judge, plan, reason, reflect, conclude template), from the task's own subgoals (a checklist derived from the instruction), or from the decision structure (a task-conditioned graph between perception and planning). Reviewers credit this as the reason the process transfers out of domain instead of memorizing. Posters that borrow annotator training or a hierarchy of lower-level attributes earn similar praise. [ids: 5VXJPS1HoM, G2kMroO9UV, 3eTr9dGwJv; poster support: bEejbORUI5, x8lg9aihwl]
- **The contribution ships a resource that measures the intermediate itself (a benchmark, step-level dataset or process metric) alongside the training recipe.** — rationale: all three orals and two spotlights bundle a dataset or benchmark with process-level evaluation (hit per step, step-level preference data, a reward-model meta-benchmark, tree data with error and correction nodes). The resource lets reviewers check that the intermediate improved, not only the final score, and it carries the paper when the RL recipe itself is called standard. [ids: JEGDp1E4OH, 3eTr9dGwJv, 5VXJPS1HoM, G2kMroO9UV, lwOV2ACEK9]
- **Credit is routed to the component that failed, or shaped per item, instead of adding one more scalar term to the outcome reward.** — rationale: the spotlight reassigns outcome reward by error source, so a wrong answer reached with sound perception is not fully penalized; another spotlight conditions the length reward and the regularizer strength on per-item difficulty. Posters with decoupled advantages per reward stream, object-level intrinsic advantages, or segment-level credit are praised for the same move. A summed reward is a common reviewer complaint on the reject side. [ids: duzdftKtUA, MI1uT5rReV; poster support: C1M4ETatgM, 7KaO3EahTO, 4fbvifBi9q]
- **The process gain comes with an outcome or efficiency gain that reviewers can see directly: higher downstream success, fewer tokens at equal accuracy, or a smaller model beating a larger one.** — rationale: the spotlights report roughly a tenth of the cost with better downstream agent success, double-digit accuracy gains with 33–67% fewer tokens, and wins over much larger models. Process-only gains without such an outcome are a recurring rejection reason (see failure modes). [ids: G2kMroO9UV, MI1uT5rReV, duzdftKtUA, lwOV2ACEK9]

## Failure modes (from Reject)
- **The intermediate reward checks surface form, text overlap or length instead of whether the intermediate is grounded or actually used.** — rationale: reviewers reject perception rewards based on lexical overlap with a reference description, rewards that constrain only tags, format and numbers so the trace may be templated text, format-only scaffolds whose mechanism goes unexplained, and depth/breadth graph scores never validated as quality measures. A description that hallucinates the right answer can still earn a high utility reward. [ids: EbfneWMXos, XOuHTBLrPP, jxZ9IYlEsG, m4Sj27sdm7, Ud9cPB9SS4, Y4DP6eWndl]
- **The method is a standard warm-start-then-RL pipeline plus one more reward term, too close to a prior or concurrent intermediate reward.** — rationale: the most frequent rejection reason. A perception reward judged too close to an accepted perception-reward paper, multi-segment grounding shaping with a contemporaneous rival, a process reward model ported from math to a new environment, and a safety pipeline mirroring a text-only predecessor all read as incremental once the closest work is named. [ids: W2pqpjROnf, UyAJQQPU4W, NKAyNK7Ljx, 9hM4YRMhfT, YjGKuOiTbR, jimY7NF6aJ, zLcTDEqDmS, TzqUDJZ23Q, Y4iaDU4yMi]
- **The intermediate improves (or is rewarded) but task accuracy barely moves, or the gain over plain outcome-only RL on the same data is not shown.** — rationale: a faithfulness reward that raises faithfulness metrics but leaves accuracy flat or negative; decoupled RL adding about 0.5 points; a map step adding about 1.6%; a consistency reward adding about 2.2 points for about 24% more compute; and ablations missing the naive answer-and-format RL baseline. [ids: CHGCkhXLFM, IlmqwQtY20, zLcTDEqDmS, hwUAApRXsC, UyAJQQPU4W, Ud9cPB9SS4]
- **The process signal comes from an unvalidated judge, teacher or external tool, forming a closed loop.** — rationale: reviewers flag LLM judges that locate mistaken turns with unverified reliability, frozen-tool reward ensembles with modest human correlation, one proprietary model acting as data generator, reward and evaluator, and learned thinking rewards whose reliability is never quantified. [ids: UtEY8lF8q2, ZZjqcpO5ga, YjGKuOiTbR, TzqUDJZ23Q, iJ4i5HE5ER, m4Sj27sdm7]
- **The reward is hand-built for one task and needs dense annotations or hand-set weights and thresholds that are not ablated.** — rationale: rewards needing ground-truth region boxes with an unweighted sum, per-anomaly hand-crafted templates, timestamp anchors usable only for one task, many hand-set retrieval weights, and linear length penalties with many tuned hyperparameters are judged ad hoc and hard to transfer. [ids: KiSsqCpppC, Jl0QHFcyCl, XOuHTBLrPP, KrKHpFquOu, wvxq3qNzHB, 7AXP2RYw2N]
- **The claimed process behavior is overclaimed relative to what the system does.** — rationale: a relation annotation called chain-of-thought that is only a data transformation; claimed multi-turn retrieval used in about 1% of cases; claimed turn-level credit where the best variant sums to the trajectory end; an inaccurate "first multi-turn" claim. [ids: tl4tD9IB0o, KrKHpFquOu, UtEY8lF8q2, 7AXP2RYw2N]

## Oral vs Reject gap
On the success side (oral ∪ spotlight, n=7), the intermediate is checked by a signal partly independent of the policy's own wording: an independent proxy reasoner, alignment to annotated or cross-verified structure, or a checklist from the instruction. Its form comes from the task or an expert procedure, and the paper ships a benchmark or step-level resource showing the intermediate itself improved, alongside a visible outcome or cost gain. Rejected papers often share the motivation and even the diagnostic (many open with an error audit showing outcome-only training leaves perception or faithfulness unimproved). They then attach a gameable check (text overlap, format, length, an unvalidated judge), present it as one more additive term in a standard warm-start-then-RL pipeline, and either fail to beat plain outcome RL on the same data or improve process metrics without moving accuracy. The diagnostic alone does not separate the tiers. What separates them is the verifiability of the check plus evidence on both the process and the outcome. With only the 3 true orals, the gap points the same way but leans more on the resource side: all three ship a benchmark with process-level evaluation, while reviewers call their training stages fairly standard (established preference and policy-gradient methods, thin training ablations), so for orals the measured, verified intermediate counts for more than the novelty of the optimizer.

## Reviewer expectations
- **Separate the gain from the intermediate reward from the gain from new data, a stronger teacher, or the base RL stage; include plain outcome-only RL on the same data.** _(source: both)_ [ids: duzdftKtUA, 3eTr9dGwJv, UyAJQQPU4W, EbfneWMXos, W2pqpjROnf, Ud9cPB9SS4]
- **Validate the checker: judge agreement with humans, extraction accuracy of the structure used as reference, sensitivity to judge strength.** _(source: both)_ [ids: duzdftKtUA, JEGDp1E4OH, UtEY8lF8q2, ZZjqcpO5ga, m4Sj27sdm7]
- **Show that the intermediate is causally used or faithful (intervention, with/without ablation, direct accuracy of the emitted evidence), not just present in the right format.** _(source: both)_ [ids: 3eTr9dGwJv, XOuHTBLrPP, jxZ9IYlEsG, Ud9cPB9SS4, OWLcXnCFdB]
- **Name the closest prior intermediate-reward or structured-reasoning work and state a decisive difference; "first" claims are checked.** _(source: both)_ [ids: 3eTr9dGwJv, JEGDp1E4OH, G2kMroO9UV, W2pqpjROnf, 7AXP2RYw2N, OWLcXnCFdB]
- **Report the training and inference cost of extra rollouts, verifiers or search, and compare at matched compute.** _(source: both)_ [ids: duzdftKtUA, lwOV2ACEK9, hwUAApRXsC, OWLcXnCFdB, wvxq3qNzHB]
- **Evaluate beyond the authors' own benchmark and beyond one backbone family.** _(source: both)_ [ids: 5VXJPS1HoM, duzdftKtUA, KiSsqCpppC, AoW258kyXp, EbfneWMXos]
- **A complete pipeline (representation, dataset, benchmark, trained model, and deployment where relevant) with ablations of each component is praised as a contribution in itself.** _(source: oral reviews)_ [ids: 3eTr9dGwJv, JEGDp1E4OH, G2kMroO9UV, 5VXJPS1HoM]

## Cognitive barriers
- Outcome correctness looks like a sufficient signal: correct answers hide wrong perception, unused evidence, or reasoning detached from the answer, and these failures only show up once each component is perturbed or audited separately (e.g., CHGCkhXLFM, hwUAApRXsC, KttCXdjj4w, duzdftKtUA).
- Intermediate supervision is assumed to need costly human step labels or a large external teacher. It is not obvious that the same policy's sufficiency check, rollout statistics, a tree with verifiable leaves, consensus of successful rollouts, or the input's own structure can stand in for those labels (e.g., C1M4ETatgM, PS8iu4PxKz, cK35kNVm5r, LuzGvybw2S, IHyY6vdYZw).
- The intermediate is treated as a fixed input or a byproduct (a fixed scene graph, an off-the-shelf caption, an instruction, a raw history) rather than as a trainable output that can carry its own reward (e.g., 3eTr9dGwJv, hlLXvyz5iP, dsQHm7YX9c, YMPYLesItf, CMU8GxwpUL).
- Learned process rewards are known to be exploitable, which deters their use. The step that unlocks them is gating the unreliable signal by the reliable outcome (trust weights, outcome-gated turn rewards, penalizing sharp drops) rather than dropping it (e.g., 0tzvmjMcXC, UhPwL6LYOc, 96I8PGPALv).
- When explicit reasoning hurts, the natural conclusion is to drop reasoning. The insight is that the form of the reasoning space is the problem (free text versus structured, checkable states or named patterns) (e.g., XtIRCAEYoJ, 5VXJPS1HoM, WGIcXH9rk9).
