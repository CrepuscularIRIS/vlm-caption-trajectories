# Add a Dedicated Carrier for Intermediate State
_id: `dedicated_state_carrier`_

**Plain alias**. _Give intermediate state its own grounded slot instead of free text_
**Definition**. Insert special learned tokens, latent states or control actions into the generation process that retrieve, reconstruct or compress input evidence, so intermediate computation is carried by grounded states rather than free-form output.
**Operational signature**. notice intermediate information is lost or verbalized poorly → add dedicated slot/action → supervise it to reconstruct or retrieve the relevant input → interleave with normal generation
**When to apply**. When long free-form intermediate outputs drift from or lose the information they are supposed to carry.
**Evidence base**. primary-tagged n=61, oral 0, spotlight 1, poster 46, reject 14; supporting-only n=19

Note on the evidence: the success side (oral ∪ spotlight) is a single spotlight (BXY6fe7q31); there are no orals among the primary papers (13 orals exist in the whole corpus). Success conditions below therefore lean on the 46 posters as supporting accepted evidence, and are marked by which side they come from. Three method lines appear twice — rejected at one venue and accepted as a poster at a later one (ZNAY3ivd62 → ViMgQW9fyt, xQIvTUL1Tf → Hn81xuLaXY, vdDtiQ1w8B → LD9mlgF5WU) — which gives an unusually clean view of what moved the decision.

## Success conditions (from Oral/Spotlight; Poster as support)
- **The information loss is diagnosed and located before the carrier is added** — The one spotlight wins on a diagnosis: the interface module keeps only content sufficient for its pre-training objective, so the fix is a feedback loop that re-queries the input using the model's own intermediate state, not more instruction data. Posters that open with a measured loss (models answering almost as well with blank inputs; a new action never adopted because the initially stronger text path dominates) get credit for a "real limitation" rather than a "new module". [ids: BXY6fe7q31, iBwW1DxQaa, VeZkY3JjWV, qmvoTiXSfG]
- **The carrier re-addresses evidence that already exists instead of generating new content** — Accepted designs retrieve precomputed input features by model-predicted address, re-inject selected existing input tokens, or train latent states to reconstruct the embeddings of query-relevant input parts. Reviewers call these simple and easy to graft on, and they often cut the default input budget at the same time (one cuts default visual tokens by ~70%). [ids: kDhAiaGzrn, O4PweOea13, j84WR5ORsC, vMpvtSmtXY, 3901vVCfo3]
- **Use of the new slot or action is conditioned on need, not rewarded unconditionally** — When the carrier is an action, accepted papers show that naive bonuses make the policy either spam the action or avoid it. They fix this with bonuses that pay only on correct trajectories, penalties on the request rate, or rewards that depend on whether a case truly needs extra evidence, and they show the training dynamics. [ids: xUyMXkI958, nsNpsCpVG1, R6m6bNnmWm, 1TXn0PeBZJ, VeZkY3JjWV]
- **Gains are shown at a matched or reduced input budget** — The carrier's case is strongest when it does better with less raw input: higher accuracy from ~20 selectively inspected units than from 512 uniform ones, 30–40% fewer inputs, or full fidelity requested only on demand. In the resubmission pair, adding budget-matched efficiency results is part of what turned a reject into a poster. [ids: nsNpsCpVG1, 1TXn0PeBZJ, R6m6bNnmWm, kDhAiaGzrn, LD9mlgF5WU]
- **A control separates the carrier's contribution from tool access or data** — Accepted papers that convince reviewers compare against baselines with the same tool setup, keep training data identical across arms, or run a diagnostic that rules out memorization (such as a cursor-in-box test). Posters without such controls are praised for results but still flagged for attribution. [ids: coJqVkqb03, iBwW1DxQaa, dqmt0Yd4rc, nsNpsCpVG1]
- **Breadth across benchmarks, backbones or encoders** — Posters that clear the bar test on many benchmarks and more than one backbone or encoder, or show that scaling holds (baselines get worse as reasoning grows longer while the carrier keeps improving). Rejected carriers are often tested on a single domain. [ids: gCWLkqK45O, Lm3Js8viIP, 5IA4cx8Gzz, qmvoTiXSfG, xUyMXkI958]

## Failure modes (from Reject)
- **The work is a domain port of a crowded recipe with no technical insight beyond the port** — Most rejects add a think-act-rethink loop, latent tokens, an interleaved sketch or a learned crop policy to a new domain or input type. Reviewers then find the warm-start-then-policy-gradient recipe and the carrier itself already in prior work, and the meta-review calls it "incremental" or "an engineering improvement". [ids: JOhD3q112L, ofNbGPV6Ve, MHZaDAoYru, Srcsec50YK, xQIvTUL1Tf, YYGsM9ZHHB, ZNAY3ivd62]
- **The diagnosed loss is never causally tied to the gain** — The paper shows the information decays (or a shortcut exists) but the carrier barely beats a simpler variant, or the separation it claims is backed only by qualitative attention maps. Reviewers then doubt that the diagnosed loss is what limits performance. [ids: LF9bRCLHMW, 9EUnRQVf3S, Srcsec50YK, xQIvTUL1Tf]
- **Evaluation is too narrow for the claimed generality** — One synthetic dataset, math-only benchmarks, or a single base model, while the paper frames the carrier as a general reasoning mechanism. [ids: LF9bRCLHMW, Srcsec50YK, MHZaDAoYru, xQIvTUL1Tf, 9EUnRQVf3S]
- **Baselines are weak, and data is not separated from method** — Rejects compare against trivial or outdated baselines, or against baselines that never see the new data or structured information, so reviewers cannot tell what the carrier adds. [ids: AJQuTFd9es, k0qTnbQxzR, JYjVCFgHYI, JOhD3q112L, xQIvTUL1Tf]
- **The extra inference cost is not accounted for** — Serial reason-retrieve-reason loops or added latent tokens raise latency (about 8x in one case, ~70% in another) with no compute-matched comparison. A learned multi-step policy that mostly calls its action once also undercuts the iteration claim. [ids: vdDtiQ1w8B, MHZaDAoYru, ZNAY3ivd62]
- **The carrier depends on an external component or unverified generated supervision that limits generality** — Reviewers reject carriers fed by a detector trained on synthetic data, or by graphs and question-answer data generated by a proprietary model with no human check. [ids: YYGsM9ZHHB, JYjVCFgHYI, KWl9QchC1a, k0qTnbQxzR]
- **Absolute performance is below the state of the art, with overclaims** — A carrier that improves its own base model but stays far below same-size state-of-the-art models, or that claims reasoning benefits without the ablation that would show them. [ids: ofNbGPV6Ve, AJQuTFd9es, ZNAY3ivd62]

## Oral vs Reject gap
confidence: low
The success side is one spotlight. That spotlight differs from the rejects in one main way: it traces the information loss to a specific cause (the interface's training objective) and closes it with a cheap feedback loop trained on synthetic discriminative data. Most rejects instead start from a known recipe and move it to a new domain. With posters as support, the gap is mostly about how the evidence is packaged, not about the carrier itself. The same carrier types (latent reconstruction, pointer/re-injection, learned crop or zoom actions, iterative judges) appear on both sides. The accepted ones add four things: compute- or budget-matched efficiency, a control that separates the carrier from data and tools, more than one backbone or benchmark family, and direct differentiation from the closest prior line. The three resubmission pairs show this most directly. In each, essentially the same method was rejected at one venue and accepted as a poster at a later one after the rebuttal added budget-matched efficiency results, missing citations, and baselines or diagnostics. Venue and reviewer pools differ, though, so this is suggestive rather than controlled. The oral-only version cannot be assessed (n_oral = 0).

## Cognitive barriers
- The loss is blamed on the wrong component. Weak downstream behavior is read as a reasoning-capacity, language-bias or model-size problem, when the cause is that the interface or intermediate channel throws information away. Seeing that the carrier, not the reasoner, needs fixing is the first step (BXY6fe7q31, YYGsM9ZHHB, kDhAiaGzrn).
- The input is treated as fixed, encode-once context. Once it is summarized, nobody frames revisiting it as something the generator can do mid-stream, so budget limits and forgetting are accepted as unavoidable (O4PweOea13, LD9mlgF5WU, oVDAfLuRie, r2g8TV4nJy).
- Intermediate computation is assumed to need language. Because input embeddings and generated tokens share one space, latent states can be supervised to reconstruct the input directly. This option is overlooked because next-token training gives no obvious way to supervise continuous states (j84WR5ORsC, Hn81xuLaXY, LF9bRCLHMW, Srcsec50YK).
- A new slot or action seems to need only demonstrations plus an outcome reward. In practice the unfamiliar action fails at first and gets suppressed, or unconditional bonuses cause it to be spammed, so the reward has to be conditioned on need (VeZkY3JjWV, nsNpsCpVG1, xUyMXkI958, 1TXn0PeBZJ).
- Outputs are seen only as end products. Treating the model's own structured or numeric outputs as reusable inputs, or giving continuous quantities their own token space instead of serialized text, means reframing output as state (Pm1NXHgzyf, dqmt0Yd4rc, e3geukCBw6, Lm3Js8viIP).
