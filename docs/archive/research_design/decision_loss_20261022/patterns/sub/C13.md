# C13 — Process-Level Reasoning Reward Shaping
_parent: **Make Intermediate Steps Explicit and Score Them** (`reward_explicit_intermediates`)_ · secondary: Repair a Degenerate Learning Signal · n=15 (oral 0, spotlight 1, poster 10, reject 4)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to treat the reasoning process itself — not only the final answer — as the object the reward governs: how long it is allowed to be, whether it is needed at all, whether it is self-sufficient, what style it takes, or how closely it conforms to reference steps. The recurring instruments are: a per-sample reasoning budget set from an external difficulty estimate combined with the policy's own uncertainty, enforced by a soft overrun penalty inside a group-relative reward; a learned style judge, trained on paired verbose and concise outputs for the same problems, used as a small auxiliary reward in place of a scalar length threshold; a sufficiency test in which the same policy is re-prompted with only its own intermediate output, and a correct downstream answer certifies that intermediate, with that reward stream given its own advantage; a frozen expert module placed in the loop so the policy's emitted intermediate is scored by the expert's resulting output; staged rewards that first check the reasoning and then the outcome; counterfactual filtering that keeps only training instances whose answer changes when the key evidence is removed, so the reward favors evidence-driven reasoning; and, at the extreme, an ablation that removes the reasoning segment and rewards the answer alone to test whether the process was ever needed. The load-bearing artifact is the process reward plus evidence that it changes the process (fewer tokens, self-contained intermediates, evidence dependence) without trading away accuracy.

## Step-by-Step

1. First establish whether, and where, the reasoning process matters for the task. Compare the standard recipe against an answer-only variant, blank or reposition the reasoning segment at inference, and identify which cases need extended reasoning; the spotlight-tier paper's whole contribution was a counter-intuitive demonstration that an assumed process ingredient was not what drove the gain. Shaping a process you have not shown to be necessary or harmful leaves the reward without a justified target.
2. Define the process property you will reward and make it checkable without the evaluand grading its own story. Use a re-prompting sufficiency test, a frozen expert's output, a counterfactual intervention on the input, or a judge trained on paired contrasts of the same problem — and when the same policy acts as verifier, show with evidence that the intermediate faithfully reflects the input rather than merely convincing the policy. Reviewers raised circularity whenever the difficulty labels, judge and policy came from one model family, and a reject's explainability metric depended on an unvalidated external oracle.
3. Keep process and outcome signals separable in the optimizer. Give the intermediate reward its own advantage or a decoupled loss, stage a reasoning-check reward before the outcome reward, or anchor the process term so it cannot dominate correctness; make budget penalties soft and adaptive per sample rather than uniform. A reject whose advance over its baseline was mainly an added linear length penalty with many hand-tuned constants was judged a limited methodological contribution.
4. Measure the process change in end-to-end terms and explain it. Report latency, throughput or compute alongside token counts, account for extra rollouts the process reward requires, and explain why shorter or self-contained reasoning helps rather than only showing that it does; include a no-reasoning control. Rejected and borderline papers were faulted for token-only efficiency evidence, unreported second-rollout cost, and unexplained accuracy gains from shorter reasoning.
5. Position against the nearest process-reward recipe and validate beyond one backbone and one in-distribution set. Compare to supervised fine-tuning and to existing length-control or step-conformity methods at matched tuning, ablate each reward component and weight, and test on an established evaluation set outside your own construction. Three of the four rejects were read as repackaging a standard warm-start-then-policy-gradient recipe with standard checks, on a single backbone and a small self-built evaluation set.

## Differentiation within parent

- vs **Credit the Localization Subgoal** (C01): that sibling makes one specific subgoal — where to look — explicit and rewards it directly or by enabling the answer. Here the reward targets properties of the reasoning trace as a whole (budget, necessity, style, self-sufficiency, step conformity), regardless of what the trace is about.
- vs **Reward the Evidence Stage Separately** (C05): that sibling forces a distinct observation segment before reasoning and scores its fidelity, coverage or consistency against the input. Here the evidence segment, when present, is only one way to govern the process; the distinguishing questions are how much reasoning to spend and whether it is needed at all.
- vs **Checkable Intermediate Artifacts as Rewards** (C12): that sibling requires the trace to emit machine-checkable artifacts (restatements, regions, numeric anchors) and rewards their correctness. Here most process rewards are not correctness checks on an artifact but shaping terms on length, style, sufficiency or conformity, so the risk shifts from artifact verification to circular or hackable process signals.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when outcome-trained reasoning is demonstrably wasteful or decoupled from the answer — overthinking on easy cases, verbose traces, intermediates that do not suffice on their own, or a process the task may not need — and you can construct a process signal that is checkable without the policy grading its own narrative. If you cannot show the process matters, or your only process signal is a hand-tuned length penalty, this collapses into an incremental reward tweak.

## Tactical failure mode

Papers attempting this tactic fail most often when the process reward is a thin addition to a standard recipe — a length penalty or relevance term bolted onto an unchanged group-relative optimizer — with many manually tuned constants and no ablation of each component. A second recurring shape is circular process supervision: difficulty labels, judges, reference steps or explainability scores produced by a large external model (often the same family as the policy) and never validated against humans. A third is efficiency claimed in tokens only, with second-rollout or encoding cost unreported and no account of why shorter reasoning helps. Finally, single-backbone, small in-distribution evaluation without supervised or established-evaluation baselines caps even sensible designs. Self-check: has the necessity of the process been tested by removing it; is the process signal independent of the policy's own self-assessment; and is the change measured in end-to-end cost against the closest existing process-reward method.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Removing the reasoning segment and rewarding only answer equality, then blanking or moving the segment after the answer at inference, can show that an assumed ingredient was not driving the gain — a diagnostic result valued at the spotlight tier.
- Re-prompting the same policy with only its intermediate output turns "is this intermediate self-sufficient" into a dense, annotation-free reward, provided that reward is given its own advantage separate from the final-answer stream.
- Setting each sample's reasoning budget from external difficulty and the policy's own uncertainty, enforced by a soft penalty, compresses easy cases while preserving hard ones, and was credited when backed by latency gains and a no-reasoning control.
- A judge trained on paired verbose versus concise outputs of the same problems scores style rather than length, giving a more precise conciseness signal than a token threshold.
- Placing a frozen expert module in the loop to score the policy's emitted intermediate via the expert's output removes the need for reasoning-annotated supervision.
- Keeping only training instances whose answer changes when the key evidence is removed, then scheduling data by reward variance, cuts data and cost while pushing the process toward evidence rather than priors.

### Reject lessons
- Adding a brevity-plus-relevance process reward while dropping the reference model was seen as a direct application of existing techniques when it failed to beat simpler classifiers on a small self-built evaluation set.
- An adaptive length budget whose main advance was a linear overrun penalty with many hand-tuned hyperparameters, evaluated only by token counts and on one backbone, was judged a limited contribution.
- Rewarding conformity to reference reasoning steps produced by a large oracle model, with an unvalidated explainability metric and no per-reward ablation, was read as an incremental recipe.
- A supervised warm-start followed by a policy-gradient stage rewarding discretized targets after reasoning was rejected as a combination of existing parts in a now-standard pipeline.

_evidence note: one spotlight and no oral; four rejects; the cluster mixes length/budget control with a few looser members (tool-parameter policies, language-space experience editing), so lessons weight the budget and sufficiency papers._
