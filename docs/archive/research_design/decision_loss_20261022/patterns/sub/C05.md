# C05 — Reward the Evidence Stage Separately
_parent: **Make Intermediate Steps Explicit and Score Them** (`reward_explicit_intermediates`)_ · secondary: Decompose Into Separately Verifiable Parts · n=23 (oral 0, spotlight 1, poster 14, reject 8)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to argue that an outcome-only reward cannot tell whether a failure came from misreading the input or from faulty reasoning over it, and then to give the input-reading stage its own credit. Usually the output is restructured so an explicit evidence or observation segment precedes reasoning, and that segment is scored on its own terms: fidelity to a reference description, coverage of key cues, consistency with the subsequent reasoning, factual grounding of stated observations against dense source descriptions, a level-by-level match to a structured rationale, or — in the strongest form — *sufficiency*, judged by whether an independent proxy solver that never sees the raw input can answer from the written evidence alone. Rewards are then routed or combined so that credit depends on both correct reading and a correct answer (multiplicative gating, gating on answer correctness, or softening the penalty when evidence was sufficient but the answer wrong). A second family makes the evidence stage accountable without writing it out: an objective term rewards the output's sensitivity to corrupting the input, or a reward pays for output divergence between an input and a transformed counterpart that should change the answer, or verifiable pretext tasks built from the input's own structure supply perception-only rewards. Nearly every paper opens with a diagnostic — an error audit showing reading errors dominate after outcome-based training, a reasoning-drift analysis, an insensitivity probe — to justify the separate signal.

## Step-by-Step

1. Prove with a diagnostic that the reading stage, not the reasoning stage, is the residual bottleneck. Run an error audit after outcome-based optimization, a with-versus-without-reasoning comparison showing traces drift from the evidence, or an insensitivity test (an order-reversed or corrupted input leaves outputs unchanged). Accepted papers foreground this measurement; it is what licenses a separate reward instead of a generic reward tweak.
2. Make the evidence stage separately verifiable with a check that measures what the next stage needs, not surface overlap. Prefer sufficiency (can an independent solver answer from the evidence alone), factual grounding against the source, or a reward intrinsic to the input (sensitivity to corruption, divergence under a transformation that should change the answer, verifiable structural pretext tasks) over text-overlap similarity to a reference. Rejects were faulted for overlap-based perception rewards that do not verify grounding, and for utility rewards that pay a description which hallucinates the right answer.
3. Route credit by error source and couple the evidence reward to the outcome deliberately. Decide whether a wrong answer with sound evidence should be penalized less, whether evidence credit is gated on answer correctness, or whether both must hold multiplicatively; justify the weights. Arbitrary or contradictory reward weights, unvalidated consistency judges and ablations in which removing a reward component helps were recurring reject signals.
4. Isolate the evidence reward's contribution from data, judge and base-recipe effects. Compare against plain outcome-and-format training on the same data, run stage-wise ablations, and address the dependence on a stronger external describer or judge (reliability checks, cost accounting, a version without it). Concerns that gains came from new synthesized data or distillation from a stronger reference source appear on nearly every accepted paper and were decisive for several rejects.
5. Show the separately rewarded evidence translates into task accuracy, and differentiate from existing perception-reward work. Report accuracy alongside faithfulness or grounding metrics, test more than one backbone and beyond the training domain, and state precisely how the reward differs from the nearest prior perception or grounding reward. A diagnostic paper whose method improved faithfulness but not accuracy, and a method judged too close to an existing perception reward, were both rejected.

## Differentiation within parent

- vs **Credit the Localization Subgoal**: that sibling rewards *where to look* — a locate-then-answer split, often coarse-to-fine or tool-driven, credited by localization quality or by whether the located region enables the answer. This card rewards *what was read* — a written or implicit evidence stage scored for fidelity, coverage, consistency or sufficiency — and need not involve any spatial selection or tool action.
- vs **Checkable Intermediate Artifacts as Rewards**: that sibling requires machine-checkable intermediates (regions, assertions, numeric anchors, entity values) verified by rule or match. Here the evidence segment is usually free-form and is scored indirectly — by a proxy solver, a judge, a reference description or an input-contrast signal — because the reading stage rarely has an exact checkable form.
- vs **Process-Level Reasoning Reward Shaping**: that sibling governs the reasoning process itself (its length, quality, conformity to reference steps, or whether reasoning is needed at all). This card leaves reasoning to the outcome reward and singles out the upstream evidence stage, with credit routed by whether failure was in reading or in reasoning.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when outcome-based training has already repaired much of the reasoning but an audit shows residual failures concentrate in reading the input, or when the system's outputs are provably insensitive to input changes that should matter. It fits best when you can verify the evidence stage by something stronger than wording overlap — a proxy solver, source-grounded checks, or an input-contrast signal. If your only check is text similarity to a reference from another model, or you cannot separate the reward's effect from the new data used to train it, the contribution will read as a data or distillation recipe.

## Tactical failure mode

Attempts fail most often by scoring the evidence stage with a weak proxy — overlap with a reference description, an unvalidated judge, or downstream utility without a faithfulness constraint — so the reward can be satisfied by wording or by a hallucinated shortcut rather than by correct reading. A second recurring shape is attribution failure: the evidence reward arrives bundled with new synthesized data, a stronger reference describer and a warm-start stage, and without a same-data outcome-only baseline or stage-wise ablation the gain cannot be credited to separate rewarding. A third is novelty collision: describe-first formats with a perception reward already exist, and a variant whose differences look like implementation details is judged too close. A fourth is a disconnect between the measured property and the task — faithfulness or grounding metrics improve while accuracy stays flat or drops, or gains are confined to one domain and backbone. Self-check: does a diagnostic show reading is the residual bottleneck; does the evidence check verify sufficiency or grounding rather than wording; and does the gain survive a same-data, outcome-only baseline while improving task accuracy.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Scoring written evidence by whether an independent solver that never sees the raw input can answer from it alone, and softening the penalty when evidence is sufficient but the answer wrong, separates "bad seeing" from "bad thinking" — the framing reviewers called fundamental.
- A manual error audit showing most post-training failures are reading errors motivates a drop-in objective that rewards an output's likelihood drop when the input is corrupted, requiring no extra data, reward models or teachers.
- Demonstrating that stepwise reasoning drifts from the evidence toward priors, then adding a judged evidence-consistency reward and supplying stage-wise ablations, moves a borderline paper to acceptance.
- Using the natural contrast between an input and a transformed counterpart that should change the answer, and rewarding divergence between the outputs, makes an invisible insensitivity both measurable and trainable.
- Checking whether stated intermediate observations match dense source descriptions penalizes hallucinated steps directly and is accepted when paired with evidence that the data and reward each contribute.
- Repurposing a structural ordering pretext task as a verifiable perception reward gives annotation-free supervision of the input itself, provided it is shown on more than one base system with variance.

### Reject lessons
- A perception reward computed as text overlap with a reference description does not verify grounding and may reward wording overfit; with an unfair base comparison and an outdated backbone, it was rejected.
- A describe-first format with a judged perception reward multiplied into the outcome reward was judged too close to an existing perception reward, with the differences reading as implementation details.
- Rewarding a description by its counterfactual utility for the answer, with no faithfulness constraint, lets a description that hallucinates the answer earn high reward, and small gains over plain outcome training did not survive review.
- A diagnostic that exposes ignored intermediate evidence is valued, but a method that raises faithfulness metrics without improving task accuracy, and without explaining the gap, is rejected.
- Coverage and consistency rewards whose removal sometimes helps, with no plain format-and-answer baseline, read as complex reward engineering with marginal, inconsistent gains.
