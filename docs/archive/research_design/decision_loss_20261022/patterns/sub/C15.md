# C15 — Constrain Updates to Preserve Knowledge
_parent: **Constrain Which Parameters May Change** (`constrain_what_changes`)_ · secondary: none · n=30 (oral 0, spotlight 0, poster 19, reject 11)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to treat forgetting or task interference during adaptation as a problem of *where* the update is allowed to land, and to decide that location from a cheap, explicit estimate of what the existing system already depends on. Authors build a per-parameter, per-layer, per-subspace or per-module importance signal — weight magnitude contrasted against live gradients, activation statistics on a small calibration or self-generated probe set, the covariance of old activations, curvature or Fisher information, spectral components of a weight difference, a dominant representational axis — and then translate it into a structural restriction: a sparse update mask, a null-space or orthogonal initialization, a reset-to-pretrained patch, a per-subspace gate, an interpolation coefficient, or a set of separated and routed modules that keeps tasks in distinct parameter regions. A recurring and well-received variant first *decomposes* the forgetting itself (format drift versus true knowledge loss, assignment drift versus component overwrite, a paradigm effect versus a data-distribution effect) with a freeze-one-retrain-the-other or swap-one-factor diagnostic, then attaches a targeted constraint to each separated cause. Reported results pair new-task gain with retention on held-out prior capabilities, usually through a combined stability/plasticity indicator.

## Step-by-Step

1. Separate the forgetting into named causes before constraining anything. Run a controlled diagnostic — freeze one component and retrain another, diversify output format to expose whether apparent loss is surface drift, or hold the algorithm fixed and swap only the training-data distribution — so each failure has its own locus. Accepted work earns its framing here; rejects that never investigated why their observed phenomenon occurred were faulted for a weak link between phenomenon and design.
2. Derive the importance signal from something the system already exposes, and justify why it tracks stored capability. Use frozen-weight versus gradient contrast, forward activation statistics, old-activation covariance, curvature, or spectral structure of an update, computed cheaply and, where possible, without the original training data. Validate the proxy directly (random-mask and alternative-score controls, sensitivity to the calibration set and layers); an importance score that is only indirectly validated, or that looks like a recombination of known pruning heuristics, is the most common novelty and soundness objection.
3. Convert the signal into a structural restriction tied to the diagnosed cause: a static sparse mask, null-space or orthogonal initialization, a reset-plus-compensated patch, per-subspace gates, interference-triggered capacity expansion, or task-separated routed modules. Show that the specific restriction — not merely fewer trainable parameters or added capacity — produces the retention, for instance by checking that routing actually uses multiple modules or that the constrained subspace beats an equally sized unconstrained one.
4. Evaluate retention and acquisition jointly under a realistic adaptation regime and against tuned baselines. Use multiple task orders or a genuinely sequential stream rather than a single adaptation step, include established continual-learning and merging baselines with a fair hyperparameter budget, report variance across seeds, and account for memory and compute (rankings, covariance, Hessian or multiple passes). Rejects repeatedly cited single-step evaluation, reused baseline numbers, under-tuned full fine-tuning and missing cost accounting.
5. Bound the claim to what the restriction is shown to protect, and make it more than a generic transplant. State the capacity limits (null-space depletion over many rounds, saturating dictionaries, a hand-chosen retention ratio or merge coefficient) and supply a principled selection rule for any such knob. If the constraint would apply identically to a single-modality system, demonstrate what the multi-stream setting specifically requires; a generic technique relocated without setting-specific design was judged to have thin novelty.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when adaptation measurably erodes prior capability or when jointly trained tasks interfere, and you can estimate — cheaply and without the original training data if necessary — which parameters, subspaces or modules carry the capability to keep. It is strongest when a diagnostic can split the observed forgetting into distinct mechanisms so each restriction has a stated target. If the only lever you have is fewer trainable parameters, or the importance proxy cannot be checked against a random or alternative selection, the contribution will read as an efficiency tweak rather than a retention mechanism.

## Tactical failure mode

Attempts fail most often as a recombination of known pieces — an importance heuristic, an adapter, a null-space projection, a routed mixture of modules — relocated into a new setting without evidence that the specific restriction is what causes the retention; reviewers then point to closely related prior constraints, flat results across retention ratios, or routing that collapses to a couple of modules. A second recurring shape is a thin evaluation regime: a single backbone, one or two tasks, a single adaptation step instead of a sequence, and baselines that are reused or under-tuned, so neither the generality nor the stability claim is established. A third is an unverified causal story — a mechanism inferred from attention scores, a balance coefficient or a fitted scaling law whose link to final performance is assumed, or theory proved only in a toy setting and carried over to the full system. Self-check: has the forgetting been decomposed so the constraint has a named target; is the importance proxy shown to beat random and alternative selection; and does the retention hold across a real sequential stream against tuned, budget-matched baselines.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Splitting measured forgetting into superficial format drift and genuine knowledge loss — by diversifying output styles within each task — lets a constraint be applied only where real loss remains, and the decomposition itself is credited as a new lens.
- A freeze-one-component, retrain-the-other diagnostic that separates assignment drift from component overwriting gives each stabilizer a clear target and is praised as clean motivation.
- Contrasting a frozen prior-importance signal with live task-gradient importance yields a parameter-free update mask that reviewers accept as a novel, architecture-agnostic selection rule.
- Initializing a low-rank update in the null space of old-activation covariance separates acquisition from retention, and pairing it with a balanced acquisition/retention metric strengthens the case.
- Holding the optimization algorithm fixed and swapping only the training-data distribution isolates *why* one adaptation paradigm forgets less, turning a known observation into an actionable recipe (train on the model's own filtered outputs).
- Re-expressing new targets in the representation the backbone already assigns high probability can make a low-rank update sufficient, locating forgetting in a data-level mismatch rather than in missing regularization.

### Reject lessons
- Transplanting an importance criterion from another subfield and freezing the top-ranked parameters, evaluated on one backbone and a single adaptation step, reads as limited novelty — especially when the metric is nearly flat across retention ratios.
- A constraint that closely resembles an existing null-space or projection method, without a direct head-to-head comparison, is judged insufficiently novel even when the overall results are strong.
- A generic stability/plasticity technique that would apply unchanged to a single-modality system is faulted for lacking setting-specific design.
- Reporting a striking phenomenon (collaboration underperforming isolated training, modality imbalance) without investigating its cause leaves the proposed separation of parameter groups only weakly linked to it.
- Proposing a balance coefficient, scaling law or attention-based mechanism whose connection to final performance is never directly shown — with theory restricted to simplified settings — leaves the convergence or causal claims unsupported.
