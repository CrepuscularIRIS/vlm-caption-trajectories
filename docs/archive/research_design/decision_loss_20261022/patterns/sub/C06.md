# C06 — Symbolic-Proxy Data Synthesis
_parent: **Synthesize Supervision From a Privileged Exact Representation** (`privileged_proxy_synthesis`)_ · secondary: none · n=49 (oral 2, spotlight 0, poster 28, reject 19)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to sidestep the target model's weak perception during data creation: obtain an exact, text-readable stand-in for each hard-to-process input — the program or specification that rendered it, its underlying symbolic structure, the synthetic labels used to degrade it, dense human-written descriptions, a tool's output, structured scene or relation annotations, a reconstruction-derived measurement — hand that stand-in (not the raw input) to a strong text-only generator, and let it author instructions, questions, rationales or dialogues at scale that are correct by construction. The target model is then trained on the raw input paired with this supervision, usually with an unchanged backbone and a light staged recipe (align, then instruct). Variants differ in where the stand-in comes from (render-from-program so labels are lossless; inverse-trace real examples into templates and re-instantiate; reuse existing annotations reformatted into a missing interaction format; pseudo-label unlabeled inputs with a specialist and filter), in how the generator is constrained (inject known labels so it narrates rather than judges; split extraction from writing; evolve instructions along controlled complexity axes; rubric-gated generator plus a separate verifier), and in what the synthesized data targets (a missing format, a scarce content type, a new skill, or a decomposed set of atomic skills). The headline evidence is that a model trained on proxy-authored data closes a gap that general models leave open, ideally on inputs and evaluators independent of the synthesis pipeline.

## Step-by-Step

1. Pin the gap to missing supervision, not missing capacity, and find the exact stand-in that makes supervision free. Show that general models fail because the training data lacks a format, content type or skill, then identify a representation that fully specifies the input and can be read as text — a generating program, symbolic structure, known synthetic labels, dense human descriptions or existing structured annotations. The stand-in must be lossless for the property you supervise; summaries that drop perceptual content, or pseudo-labels with large residual error, propagate their gaps into every generated item.
2. Constrain the generator so it only writes what the stand-in guarantees. Feed the exact labels or program into the prompt so the generator narrates rather than perceives; decompose generation into extraction then grounded writing; ground every rewriting round in per-sample context and eliminate drifted or ungrounded rewrites; or separate an independent verifier from the generator with explicit pass/fail gates. Reject designs repeatedly let a single model generate, filter and judge, or produced formulaic, low-diversity templated items — both read as unsound construction.
3. Engineer controllable diversity and realism into the synthesis. Expose independent knobs for structural complexity, rendering style and difficulty; render one specification through several back-ends or an optimizing layout engine; induce templates from real examples rather than generic generators; and add realistic degradations. Purely clean, single-renderer or single-source synthetic data is the most frequent rejection cause because transfer to noisy, hand-made or real-world inputs is never demonstrated.
4. Train with minimal architectural change and isolate the data's contribution. Keep the backbone and use a simple staged recipe; then prove the gain comes from proxy-authored supervision rather than more data or a stronger generator: compare against matched-size data from the seed set or prior pipelines, fine-tune baselines on the same data when fairness requires it, and decompose recognition versus reasoning gains. Reviewers flagged unmatched data volume, untuned baselines and unseparated components as the reason "the dataset just adds data" stuck.
5. Validate on inputs and judges that are independent of the synthesis loop. Report human audits or verifier agreement on a sample of generated items, check overlap with evaluation sets, evaluate on real or authentic-distortion inputs and on third-party evaluation suites, and avoid scoring with the same model family that generated the data. Where the contribution is the corpus, add a measurable insight — a decomposition into transferable atomic skills, a diagnosed train-test mismatch, a controlled difficulty axis — so the work is more than an incremental data extension.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when the input is hard for current models to perceive but you can cheaply obtain an exact textual or programmatic specification of it at data-creation time, and the target skill is under-represented in existing training data. If the only available stand-in is lossy or noisy, if you cannot show transfer beyond your own renderer or source, or if the generator, filter and evaluator must all be the same model, the result will be read as an incremental synthetic dataset.

## Tactical failure mode

Papers attempting this tactic fail most often on the synthetic-to-real gap: training and test data come from the same generator, renderer or source domain, and gains on authentic, noisy or out-of-domain inputs are small or untested. A second recurring shape is circular quality assurance — one model generates the data, filters it and judges the outputs, with only a tiny human-checked fraction — so data correctness is asserted rather than measured. A third is "just more data": the method is an existing architecture fine-tuned on an extended pipeline, compared against baselines trained on less or different data, so the contribution reduces to scale and the result is called expected. A fourth is overclaiming — calling a training-data transformation "reasoning", or claiming universality when only some sub-skills transfer. A candidate can self-check: is the stand-in lossless for the supervised property, is the data's quality verified independently of the generator, and does the gain survive on real inputs at matched data size?

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Representing a new input type as symbolic text lets a text-only generator author diverse conversations, descriptions and reasoning questions for it, so instruction following can be bootstrapped with only a light connector and no human annotators.
- Decomposing a broad judging skill into a few atomic sub-skills, synthesizing positive/negative pairs for each, and showing which ones transfer gives an actionable insight beyond the dataset itself, especially when critiques are turned into concrete edit instructions.
- When the input's underlying structured annotation is lossless, it can fully replace perception during data generation, yielding accurate descriptions and many problem variants at a scale far beyond hand-labeled sets.
- A parametric synthesizer with independent controls for structure and rendering style can serve both as training data and as a difficulty-stratified diagnostic, provided cross-domain or real-input evidence is added.
- Inducing reusable templates from real examples through a human-in-the-loop loop and re-instantiating them programmatically produces samples paired with exact underlying data while staying close to real-world design.
- A generator constrained by a machine-checkable schema and filtered by a distinct rubric-driven verifier makes synthesized data auditable, and contamination analysis plus stage ablations make the pipeline credible.

### Reject lessons
- A purely synthetic, clean, renderer-constrained dataset with no demonstrated transfer to scanned, noisy or authentic inputs is judged lacking real-world relevance, even with large in-distribution gains.
- When one proprietary model generates descriptions, questions, training data and the evaluation set, and also acts as judge, with only about one percent human-checked, the central claims are treated as circular and unsupported.
- Extending an existing instruction-data pipeline to a new content type with an unchanged architecture, where the finding is that more such data helps, is read as an expected, incremental dataset contribution.
- Comparing a model trained on several times more evolved data against a seed-set baseline, with no matched-size control or comparison to other generation methods on the same seed, leaves the gain unattributable.
- Synthesizing data with no quality control, no human validation of generated items and unreliable model judges leaves data quality unestablished and dominates the decision regardless of the creative construction.
