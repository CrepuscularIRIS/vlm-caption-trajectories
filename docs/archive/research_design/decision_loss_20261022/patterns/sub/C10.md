# C10 — Cheap Verifiable Rewards Replace Rationales
_parent: **Substitute Cheap Verifiable Signals for Expensive Supervision** (`cheap_verifiable_proxy_reward`)_ · secondary: none · n=41 (oral 0, spotlight 3, poster 29, reject 9)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to stop paying for annotated reasoning and instead let a policy-gradient objective discover the reasoning, driven by a reward that is cheap to compute and unambiguous to check. The supervision that would have been expensive — written rationales, dense step labels, task-direct targets — is replaced by something already abundant or programmatically known: existing scalar or categorical labels, a discretized version of a continuous target, a proxy discrimination or matching task verified from side metadata, a corruption whose location is known by construction, a game or rule system that grades itself, a machine-checkable symbolic form of an explanation, or a set of executable assertions. Authors frequently combine several such signals (two complementary label types, a judgment plus a localizable rationale, a set of verifiers covering input, mediator and output of an intermediate artifact) and often bolt on a light warm-start to fix output format, or a staged/curriculum ordering from densely checkable easy sub-tasks to the sparse full task. The load-bearing artifact is the reward-construction recipe itself — what makes the signal both cheap and faithful to the target capability — and the headline result is that label-only or proxy-only reward elicits reasoning that transfers out of distribution better than supervised imitation of rationales.

## Step-by-Step

1. Name the supervision you are refusing to pay for and the reason imitation of it fails. State which annotation is costly (explanatory text, dense process labels, direct target labels) and give evidence that supervised imitation of it underperforms — memorizes surface format, collapses out of distribution, or copies the style of the reasoning without making it causally useful. The strongest submissions turn this into a diagnostic finding of its own (imitation reproduces the form of the intermediate but not its function); a bare "reinforcement is better than imitation" assumption reads as a recipe transplant.
2. Construct a reward whose correctness is known by construction and whose difficulty still covers the target capability. Options: reuse cheap scalar/categorical labels, discretize a continuous target into a few verifiable choices, pick a proxy task whose answer follows from side metadata, plant a known corruption and reward locating it, generate items from domain rules, or force the explanation into a machine-checkable symbolic form. Check explicitly that the proxy cannot be solved by a shortcut cue (shallow surface features, near-duplicate retrieved inputs, lower-dimensional cues than the capability you claim) and that precision lost by discretization does not gut the task.
3. Make the reward say something specific about the capability, not only "correct/incorrect". Combine complementary verifiable signals that support each other, cover each functional role of an intermediate artifact with its own checker, or add partial credit for verifiable sub-parts. If you include a softer learned or judged component, gate it by agreement with the verifiable outcome, anneal it, or project it so it cannot fight the primary objective — and test judge reliability and swap sensitivity, because weaker judges are where reward hacking appears. A recipe of only format-plus-accuracy rewards on a new dataset is the most common reason for a novelty rejection.
4. Isolate the reward as the cause of the gains. Run the controls reviewers ask for: a supervised baseline on the very same examples, the same algorithm with only the outcome reward, a compute- and step-matched comparison, warm-start-only and reward-only variants, and a check that a stronger trace generator or extra joint-training data is not doing the work. Use more than one backbone family and report multiple seeds; small single-family gains near noise are routinely discounted.
5. Demonstrate transfer and scope the claim to what the proxy actually exercises. Evaluate on established external sets the training reward never touched, analyze overlap between proxy sources and test items, report retention of general ability, and keep "zero-shot", "emergent" or "general reasoning" claims proportional to how far the evaluation is from the proxy. Define any claimed emergent behavior operationally and link it causally to accuracy rather than to length or keyword counts.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when the capability you want is expensive to annotate as reasoning but some cheap, unambiguous signal correlated with it already exists or can be manufactured by construction (labels, metadata, known corruptions, rules, executable checks), and when you can argue — ideally show — that imitation of rationales fails in a specific way that the reward fixes. If the only available reward is a coarse correctness flag on a new niche dataset and your method is otherwise the standard warm-start-then-reinforce recipe, the contribution will read as a domain transplant.

## Tactical failure mode

The dominant failure is the recipe transplant: an existing warm-start-plus-policy-gradient pipeline with format and accuracy rewards applied to a new domain or dataset, with no reward or algorithmic insight, judged incremental regardless of solid numbers. The second is unisolated attribution — gains that could come from a stronger trace generator, extra training data, a supervised warm-start that already sees the answers, or extra optimization steps, with no same-data supervised or outcome-only control. The third is reward-validity risk: judge-based or template-based rewards whose reliability is not measured, hand-crafted per-case templates that do not scale, rewards that mirror the evaluation metric, or proxies solvable by shortcut cues. The fourth is overclaiming: informally defined "emergent" behaviors measured by length or keyword frequency, zero-shot claims on tasks close to the proxy, and conclusions from a single small model. A candidate can self-check: is the reward itself a new idea that captures the capability, have I shown the reward rather than the data or teacher causes the gain, and is my transfer claim no larger than my evaluation distance.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Cheap scalar and categorical labels from two related prediction tasks, rewarded jointly so each supports the other, can elicit interpretable reasoning and strong out-of-distribution generalization with no reference explanations at all.
- Diagnosing that supervised imitation reproduces the form of an intermediate artifact but not its causal role, then writing one verifier per functional role of that artifact (its construction, its usability, its link to the answer), turns a recipe paper into a finding.
- A corruption whose location is known by construction — one planted error in an otherwise faithful description — keeps the full perceptual difficulty while making correctness exact and cheap to check, and the skill transfers beyond the proxy.
- A proxy discrimination task verified from abundant side metadata, with hard negatives, can induce broad transfer in a supervision-scarce domain, but only convinces once the warm-start's contribution is separated from the reward's.
- Forcing an explanation into a machine-checkable symbolic form lets a rule-based score replace a judge model at a fraction of the cost, and separating reward streams with different output structure can beat joint optimization.
- Ordering training from densely checkable easy sub-tasks with partial credit to the sparse full task works as a reward-native cold start in place of supervised warm-up.

### Reject lessons
- Applying the standard warm-start-then-reinforce recipe with format and accuracy rewards to a new domain, however strong the numbers, is judged incremental application without methodological novelty.
- A learned process reward gated and annealed by intuitive heuristics, without quantitative validation of the judge's reliability, reads as an ad hoc tweak to an existing pipeline.
- Claiming an emergent reasoning behavior measured only by response length and keyword frequency, on a single small model, without a causal link to accuracy, is rejected as an unformalized central claim.
- Reference traces built from hand-crafted per-case templates raise the question of why the underlying classical tools are not used directly and limit scalability to new domains.
- Rule-generated question suites that test recognition of local features, with small or near-chance external gains, are read as format learning rather than reasoning, and "boundless" coverage claims are discounted.
