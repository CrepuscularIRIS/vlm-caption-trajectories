# C17 — Self-Generated, Verified Experience Loops
_parent: **Close a Self-Generated, Verified Experience Loop** (`closed_self_verified_loop`)_ · secondary: Synthesize Supervision From a Privileged Exact Representation · n=14 (oral 0, spotlight 1, poster 11, reject 2)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to make the agent's own interaction the data source and an automatic check the labeler: propose or explore tasks, execute them, verify each outcome or step against ground truth that is available by construction (a procedurally generated task with a rule-checkable answer, a self-built environment whose internal state is fully observable, a formal state-transition specification where correctness reduces to reachability, a tool whose output can be fed back for inspection) or against a trained verifier, then convert the verified successes and — crucially — the agent's own failures into training signal, and repeat. Variants differ in where the tasks come from (a code-generated proxy task near chance for current models; synthesized tasks over a known environment state; self-exploration filtered to a learnable curriculum; a stronger model proposing practice tasks), in how verification is grounded (environment ground truth, graph search plus execution replay, a verifier trained on corrupted positives and mined hard negatives, step-level comparison of executed alternatives), and in what is harvested (on-policy failures turned into corrective traces, erroneous prefixes replayed at controlled depth, failure-discovery trees that branch at fragile states, step-level preference pairs). The headline evidence is that the loop improves the agent without human labels, that the harvested data matches the agent's own error distribution, and that gains transfer from the synthetic or controlled setting to real environments.

## Step-by-Step

1. Choose a task source whose outcomes can be verified by construction. Prefer procedurally generated tasks, self-built fully observable environments or formal state-transition specifications where correctness is exact and difficulty is controllable, so the loop never depends on a noisy external judge for its core labels. If a learned verifier is unavoidable, train it on rule-checked positives, controlled corruptions of verified trajectories and mined hard negatives, and quantify its precision and agreement with humans — unverified judge precision is a recurring concern, and a loop whose proposer and judge are both outside closed models is read as bottlenecked by those models.
2. Harvest the agent's own failures, not curated or injected errors. Collect on-policy mistakes, turn them into corrective traces with the ground truth, replay erroneous prefixes at controlled error type and depth, or branch at fragile states to discover failures and their recoveries. The strongest accepted work showed that synthetic noise or hand-built correction data mismatches the errors an agent actually induces on itself, and aligned the training data with an evaluation suite that measures exactly those failure modes.
3. State the learning procedure precisely and match its name to what it does. Specify the formal objective, how verified outcomes enter it (filtered imitation, step-level preference pairs, policy gradient with decomposed rewards), and every component of the loop (explorer, task filter, evaluator, reward weights). A loop labeled as reinforcement learning that is effectively filtered imitation with no formal objective, or whose world model, evaluator accuracy and reward settings are under-specified, was rejected on description alone.
4. Attribute gains to the loop rather than to borrowed supervision or budget. Separate the effect of warm-start trajectories distilled from stronger models from the effect of the self-verified loop (e.g. train without trajectory supervision), compare against simpler alternatives a reviewer will name (rejection sampling, distillation from a stronger model, plain policy optimization on general data, standard online-learning baselines), report per-iteration gains, and control rollout budgets. Missing directly relevant baselines with no rebuttal left gains unattributable.
5. Demonstrate transfer beyond the self-built world and bound the claims. Evaluate on real or live environments and third-party evaluation suites, report confidence intervals, show that general ability is preserved, and analyze the synthetic-to-real gap honestly (deterministic specifications miss stochastic behavior, hand-built replicas undercut "fully automated" claims). Avoid "self-evolving" or "emergent" wording when tasks, judges or tools are supplied externally, and measure the overhead of any extra in-loop critic.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when you can construct or access an environment where outcomes are checkable automatically and cheaply — ideally exactly — but curated demonstrations, especially of error recovery, are scarce. It is most compelling when the agent's own failures are the missing data and you can measure recovery directly. If your only verifier is an unvalidated model judge, or if gains cannot be separated from distillation of a stronger teacher, the loop will be read as an established recipe applied to a new task.

## Tactical failure mode

Papers attempting this tactic fail most often when the loop's supervision actually comes from outside it — a stronger closed model proposes the tasks and judges the outcomes, so improvement reflects those models rather than the method. A second recurring shape is imprecise methodology: the update is called reinforcement learning but is filtered imitation with no formal objective, or key components (task filter, evaluator, reward settings) are under-specified, and directly relevant baselines are missing. Even among accepted papers, the dominant concerns are circularity (headline gains measured inside the authors' own synthetic environment), an unquantified verifier that may mislabel valid alternative actions and accumulate noise across iterations, and gains not isolated from teacher-distilled warm-start data. A candidate can self-check: is verification grounded in construction rather than a judge, are the harvested errors the agent's own, and does the gain survive on real environments against the simplest named alternative?

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Replaying erroneous prefixes at controlled error type and depth turns recovery into a measurable capability, and growing an interaction tree that branches at fragile states produces training data matching the agent's own compositional, delayed-detection errors — an evaluation instrument and a synthesis pipeline aligned to the same failure modes.
- Writing an environment's states, actions and transitions as an explicit specification before building it reduces correctness of every step and outcome to reachability on a known graph, replacing external judges with verification by construction.
- A trivially code-generated proxy task on which current models are near chance can supply unlimited verifiable interaction data that transfers, provided gains are shown without the expert warm-start trajectories.
- Error-plus-correction examples can be manufactured from clean successful trajectories and then refined by an online loop that collects and verifies the agent's own mistakes.
- A self-built, fully observable replica of the target environment lets task synthesis exploit ground-truth state for known answers and optimal paths, matching human-annotated data at equal scale and transferring to live settings.
- Step-level preferences from executing alternative actions and comparing their results supply supervision where no ground truth exists, and a user study of verifier-human agreement makes the signal credible.

### Reject lessons
- A loop in which a stronger closed model proposes tasks and a model judge supplies outcome-only success is treated as bottlenecked by those external models rather than demonstrating the method.
- Labeling the update as reinforcement learning when it is effectively imitation on filtered trajectories with no formal objective makes the method description inaccurate and is decisive even with positive scores.
- Online training with self-explored tasks and a modified policy-gradient objective, but without comparisons to directly relevant online-learning baselines or standard objective variants, and with the world model, evaluator accuracy and reward settings under-specified, leaves gains unattributable and contributions over-claimed.
