# C14 — Align Internals to Teacher Signals
_parent: **Align Internal Representations to a Teacher** (`distill_internal_property`)_ · secondary: Diagnose the Bottleneck, Then Target It · n=25 (oral 2, spotlight 2, poster 13, reject 8)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to locate a specific property that is missing or distorted inside the system's intermediate computation — where it attends, which input stream dominates its updates, whether one input is silently ignored, whether hidden states encode a structure a specialist encodes — measure that property with a probe or proxy that tracks downstream quality, and then add a light, targeted training-time correction that supplies it, usually by aligning internals to a teacher alongside the main objective. Teachers are varied: a model trained on a different objective with sharper attention, frozen specialist encoders whose dense features become prediction targets for selected layers, a larger model whose attention or token-relation structure is matched, a context-conditioned copy of the same system whose effect is distilled into a small adapter, compressed latent targets from a frozen tokenizer, or the system's own with-versus-without-input contrast used as a per-target relevance weight. The correction is deliberately minimal — an auxiliary loss, a removable zero-initialized side path, per-stream projections in an adapter, a restricted set of existing parameters — and nothing extra is required at inference. The strongest papers let the diagnostic overturn an assumption: the gap was input ambiguity rather than missing capacity, an aggregate score hid a stream imbalance in the update, or a capability was under-elicited rather than absent.

## Step-by-Step

1. Diagnose the missing internal property with a measurement that ties it to performance. Use a probe of intermediate representations, an attention-similarity or entropy comparison against a reference, a replace-the-input-with-noise test, or a per-stream decomposition of the update, and show the measured property correlates with downstream quality. Rejects whose foundational hypothesis rested on a single experiment with negligible effect, or whose motivation was intuitive with no mechanistic check, failed here.
2. Test the minimal explanation before building machinery. Run the plain baseline that the field assumes is insufficient — ordinary fine-tuning after removing input ambiguity, tuning only an existing small parameter set, or simply raising capacity — so the diagnosis distinguishes "absent" from "under-elicited" or "ambiguous." The top-tier work in this cluster is often the one where a minimal intervention, justified by the diagnosis, closes most of the gap.
3. Choose a teacher or reference that demonstrably carries the property and bridge any representation mismatch. Show the teacher is better on the measured property (sharper, lower-entropy attention; better correspondence; correct focus), align at the layers the probe identifies, and handle depth or space mismatch explicitly (group layer matching, a reused frozen bridge). Justify the alignment geometry or target choice with an ablation against the obvious alternative; a sophisticated distance or multi-component pipeline with small gains over a simple one is read as stitched tricks.
4. Keep the correction targeted and non-invasive, and prove it is the correction that helps. Add an auxiliary loss, a removable side path or a per-stream projection rather than new inference-time inputs, and control for added capacity, extra data and extra training compute with data-matched and capacity-matched comparisons. Recurring concerns on both sides are that gains might come from added capacity, more training or a stronger generated dataset rather than from the aligned property.
5. Validate beyond the regime the diagnosis came from and report what is preserved. Test on held-out or out-of-domain tasks, more than one backbone or teacher pair, and check that general competence and other streams are not degraded; report training overhead. Rejects relied on a single evaluation set or architecture, unfair input advantages over baselines, or teachers that did not scale; accepted work was challenged on leakage between training sources and evaluation, which should be ruled out up front.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when a system fails on a capability that some other model, encoder or configuration of the same system demonstrably handles, and you can measure the relevant internal property and show it predicts performance. It fits best when the fix can be a training-time alignment or a minimal parameter change with no inference cost. If you cannot show that the teacher is better on the measured property, or that the gain survives capacity- and data-matched controls, the work will read as generic distillation or added parameters.

## Tactical failure mode

Attempts fail most often as recombinations of known distillation, alignment or adapter components applied to a new pairing, where reviewers see the novelty as only in the combination and the gains as incremental or marginal. A second recurring shape is an unsubstantiated diagnosis: the claimed internal deficit (redundancy causing errors, imbalance harming convergence, attention divergence causing failure) is supported only correlationally or by a tiny effect, so the correction has no established target. A third is confounded or unfair evaluation — extra input information unavailable to baselines, mismatched training data, differently trained comparison systems, or a single evaluation set and architecture — which prevents attributing gains to the aligned property. A fourth is overclaiming: asserting a capability is elicited rather than newly learned, or that general ability is retained, without evidence. Self-check: is the internal property measured and linked to performance; does a minimal baseline fail where the alignment succeeds; and do the gains survive capacity-, data- and input-matched comparisons on held-out settings.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Diagnosing a specialist-versus-generalist gap as ambiguity in what is queried and under what acquisition conditions — then removing that ambiguity at the input with plain fine-tuning and sparse labels — can match specialists without new heads or losses, a surprising result that reached the top tier.
- Showing that an adapter reused from a single-stream setting is dominated by one stream's updates, a fact hidden by aggregate scores, justifies stream-specific projections with a cheap interaction in the bottleneck and earned uniformly positive reviews.
- Treating a capability as latent in the backbone and surfacing it with a removable, zero-initialized side path trained on a stronger teacher's dense targets avoids sacrificing general competence to new inference-time inputs.
- Framing adaptation to a new input regime as a domain shift lets tuning only existing normalization parameters match or beat larger adapters, with an explanatory analysis of why it works.
- A probe showing that intermediate-layer representation quality tracks downstream performance licenses directly optimizing that quality by predicting specialist features from those layers.
- Relating teacher-student attention similarity to task accuracy before designing the method explains why a distilled student inherits coarse abilities but not fine-grained ones, and motivates explicit attention alignment with a bridge for the representation gap.

### Reject lessons
- Recombining known training stages, resolution schedules, packing and quality filtering into a domain recipe, without ablating each piece or comparing against established alternatives, is judged engineering rather than research.
- A foundational causal claim — that irrelevant input content causes erroneous output — supported by a single experiment with sub-percent effects leaves the whole correction unsubstantiated.
- A small input-level bridge that only produces one fixed behavior, weakly differentiated from prompt tuning or a guard filter, is read as a filter rather than a transfer of capability.
- Attention-pattern distillation evaluated on one evaluation set, with an input the baselines lack and no comparison to the distillation literature, fails on fairness and generality.
- A distillation framework where a larger teacher does not help, tested on a single architecture against differently trained systems, does not establish that the transferred structure is what matters.
