# C12 — Checkable Intermediate Artifacts as Rewards
_parent: **Make Intermediate Steps Explicit and Score Them** (`reward_explicit_intermediates`)_ · secondary: Substitute Cheap Verifiable Signals for Expensive Supervision · n=85 (oral 2, spotlight 2, poster 58, reject 23)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to make the reasoning trace emit a typed, machine-checkable artifact — a restatement of the request, a set of cited evidence references, coordinates of a localized sub-region, executable assertions against an explicitly maintained state, numeric anchors that must refine toward the answer, explicit entity values, or atomic claim units — and then attach a check to that artifact that does not require a learned judge of free text: overlap with annotated evidence, execution against the state, consistency across equivalent variants, sufficiency when the artifact replaces the original input, or the outcome gap when the artifact is counterfactually swapped out. The check is either added to the outcome reward in a policy-gradient stage (usually after a supervised warm-start that teaches the artifact's syntax) or used to build and filter supervised traces. The strongest members treat the artifact as a single resource serving both measurement and training: the same verified intermediate that exposes a hidden failure (fragmented chains, late-trace loss of input use, rationale–answer decoupling) also becomes the training signal. The load-bearing claim is always that the final answer alone cannot distinguish grounded from shortcut reasoning, and that the explicit artifact makes the difference observable and rewardable.

## Step-by-Step

1. Show, with a diagnostic on a fixed base system, that outcome-only credit hides a specific intermediate failure. Use an oracle-selection probe, a truncation probe, a counterfactual injection, or a consistency metric that average accuracy conceals, and report the gap as evidence. Do not stop at the diagnostic: purely diagnostic studies that offer no reusable training remedy, or whose remedies are modest and unstable, are declined as insufficient contribution.
2. Choose an intermediate artifact whose correctness is checkable from construction, not from fluency. Prefer typed forms — references, coordinates, entity values, executable assertions, atomic units — over free-text rationales, and tie the artifact to an exact or annotated source (an explicit state, existing labels, a simulator, an executor). Rationales produced by an external generator and accepted without human, consistency, or causal validation are the most frequent reason faithfulness claims are rejected.
3. Make the check measure the property you claim, and guard it against gaming. A format- or keyword-only check teaches good-looking artifacts; a check computed by the same frozen model or the same extractor used for evaluation is circular; a reward that a hallucinated artifact can still satisfy via the query alone is a loophole. Use a sufficiency test (the artifact alone must yield the answer), an intervention contrast with a plausible rather than destructive counterfactual, or execution against independent state, and keep reward terms conditional so the policy does not emit artifacts regardless of need.
4. Teach the artifact syntax with a small supervised warm-start, then optimize the artifact check jointly with outcome correctness, and isolate where the gain comes from. Run controls that separate the artifact reward from the added or cleaned training data, from domain adaptation, and from extra tool access given only to the proposed system (give baselines the same data, cleaning and tools). Undisentangled data-versus-method gains and heavy multi-stage compute with marginal increments over the supervised stage are recurring rejection grounds.
5. Validate the intermediate directly and bound the scope. Report artifact accuracy (evidence overlap, step correctness, consistency) on held-out cases with an independent or human-checked reference, show transfer beyond the single task and backbone used for development, and avoid "first", "causal" or "faithful" language that the implementation does not support. Task-specific, ad hoc artifact designs evaluated only on a self-built set read as narrow engineering.

## Differentiation within parent

- vs **Credit the Localization Subgoal** (C01): C01 credits one specific subgoal — where and when to look — inside a locate-then-answer decomposition; C12 is agnostic to what the artifact is (restatements, assertions, numeric anchors, entity values, claim units) and its defining feature is a mechanical check on the artifact, not the locate-then-answer staging.
- vs **Reward the Evidence Stage Separately** (C05): C05 inserts a dedicated observation segment before reasoning and scores that segment's fidelity or sufficiency as a whole; C12 embeds checkable artifacts throughout the trace (often several per trace, interleaved with steps) and scores them by structure — overlap, execution, citation, refinement — rather than treating one evidence block as a separate stage.
- vs **Process-Level Reasoning Reward Shaping** (C13): C13 shapes properties of the reasoning process itself (length budget, overall quality, conformity to reference steps, whether reasoning is needed); C12 leaves the process free and rewards concrete artifacts the process must output, so its reward is verifiable per artifact rather than a judgment about the whole trace.

## When to pick this one

Pick this when correct final answers can be reached without the intended intermediate (via priors, shortcuts or memorized patterns) AND the intended intermediate can be expressed in a typed form with a reference you can compute cheaply — existing annotations, an explicit state, an executor, a simulator, or equivalent-variant consistency. If the only way to check the intermediate is a learned judge of free text, or the "checkable" form is just format compliance, this tactic will reward appearance rather than substance.

## Tactical failure mode

The dominant failure is a check that does not measure what the paper claims: format rewards that certify structure but not content, consistency metrics that a fabricated but self-consistent rationale passes, saliency or similarity rewards scored by the same frozen model used to evaluate, and counterfactuals so destructive that the gap reflects harm rather than reliance. A second shape is attribution collapse — the intermediate reward arrives together with new curated or teacher-generated data, extra tools, or in-domain fine-tuning the baselines lack, and the paper cannot show which part caused the gain. A third is the narrow bespoke artifact: a task-specific tag scheme or reward tuned for one task, one backbone and a self-built evaluation set, presented as a general reasoning advance. Self-check: can a wrong or hallucinated artifact still earn the reward; is the artifact's correctness validated independently of the trainer and the evaluator; and does a matched-data baseline without the artifact reward close the gap?

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Building one verified resource whose step-level annotations serve both as process metrics that expose where long multi-step chains break and as process supervision that repairs them turns an evaluation contribution into a training contribution.
- Having the system contrast its rationale on near-duplicate inputs with matched queries, then rewrite the rationale, lets its own comparison ability act as a verifier for unsupported claims without any reward model.
- Requiring an explicit intermediate structured state (positions, relations) before computing the answer, and supplying exact supervision for it from a simulator, can substitute for architectural change on tasks that are trivial once the state is known.
- Expressing intermediate reasoning as executable assertions checked against an explicitly maintained state yields dense, judge-free rewards, but the claim must stay scoped to the representation the task actually provides.
- A sufficiency check — re-answering using only the system's own extracted analysis — and a matched intervention contrast that swaps the evidence both turn "did the answer depend on the intermediate" into a computable reward, provided the counterfactual is plausible rather than destructive.
- Conditional bonuses for intermediate actions, rather than unconditional ones, prevent the policy from collapsing into emitting the artifact regardless of need.

### Reject lessons
- Rewards that check only the format of a scaffold or the numeric tags in a trace, without evidence that the surrounding reasoning is genuine, leave reviewers with an unexplained mechanism and templated output.
- Process metrics that reward longer or more comprehensive chains without validating them as quality measures, and that rely on model judges with no agreement evidence, are dismissed as unvalidated.
- Scoring faithfulness with the same frozen model that produces the reward is flagged as circular, especially when paired with an unexplained collapse on one in-domain set.
- A careful stress-test that exposes rationale–answer decoupling but offers only modest, unstable remedies is read as diagnostic rather than a contribution.
- Standard warm-start-then-reward pipelines whose only new element is a domain-specific reward, with teacher-generated rationales never validated and gains not isolated from added data, read as incremental application.
