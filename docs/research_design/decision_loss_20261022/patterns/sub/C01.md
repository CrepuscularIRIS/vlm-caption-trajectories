# C01 — Credit the Localization Subgoal
_parent: **Make Intermediate Steps Explicit and Score Them** (`reward_explicit_intermediates`)_ · secondary: Decompose Into Separately Verifiable Parts (`decompose_and_reward_parts`) · n=34 (oral 0, spotlight 1, poster 23, reject 10)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to split a task into "locate, then answer" and make the locate step an explicit, separately credited output, so the system learns where and when to look instead of being judged only on its final answer. The locate step takes recurring forms: a coarse pass over a cheap low-fidelity view that proposes a sub-region or time span, followed by a fine pass that re-reads only that part at high fidelity; a learned policy that calls narrowing tools (crop, zoom, extract, fetch higher resolution) and appends each new sub-view; a candidate generator that shrinks the search space and a reasoner that selects or rejects candidates; or a reformulation of continuous localization into something the interface emits natively (choosing among persistent identity marks drawn on the input, structural tokens grounded contrastively, attention from a dedicated token over input patches, interleaved position markers). Credit to the locate step comes from overlap with annotated regions, from a dense distance-based score instead of a sparse hit, from whether a frozen verifier can answer from the located part alone, from whether the downstream answer is correct after re-reading it, or from a retrospective signal where the fine stage's localization quality rewards the coarse stage — often with separate, token-level advantages for the locate and answer parts and an efficiency term on the number of looks. The load-bearing artifact is the definition of what makes a localization "good" and how that credit is routed; the headline result is higher accuracy on fine-detail or long inputs with fewer processed tokens.

## Step-by-Step

1. Show that a single pass cannot find the evidence and that the locate step is the real bottleneck. Measure the failure the decomposition fixes: targets that are tiny or unreadable at the budgeted fidelity, a long input where dense processing is unaffordable, an oracle showing best-candidate overlap rises sharply with more candidates, or attention being hijacked by semantic priors rather than the referred region. A clear diagnostic motivation is what reviewers credit; a decomposition introduced without it reads as one more crop-and-zoom pipeline.
2. Make localization an explicit output in a form the system can emit and verify reliably. Prefer reformulations that remove brittle number generation — selecting among pre-marked identities, structural tokens aligned to the input, attention-based pointers over input patches, candidate selection with an explicit reject option — and decide how many looks are allowed. Plan for recovery when the coarse pass misses: an escalation tool, a reject-all action, or a second look; a pipeline that fails silently when the first localization is wrong is a recurring criticism.
3. Credit the localization by whether it carries the evidence, not only by overlap with a box. Reward the located part by sufficiency (a frozen verifier or the policy can answer from it alone), by answer correctness after re-reading it, or by retrospective quality fed back from the fine stage; add a dense distance-based component to avoid early sparsity, and an efficiency term so looks are spent only when they help. Route locate and answer credit separately (token-selective advantages) rather than summing unweighted terms, and justify each weight with sensitivity analysis — an unjustified unweighted reward sum that depends on annotated boxes is a reject trigger.
4. Isolate what the locate subgoal adds over the nearest alternatives under matched conditions. Compare against the closest existing iterative-refinement, crop-and-zoom, frame-selection and candidate-retrieval methods; against training-free cropping and supervised region baselines on the same backbone; and give baselines the same candidates, hints, segmentation back end and data. Separate training gains from test-time search gains, check that the model actually uses the iterative behavior it claims (count looks per instance), and report results on more than one backbone.
5. Report the cost and validate the located regions directly. Measure added latency, extra forward passes and token savings honestly; evaluate how often the located part truly contains the evidence; analyze recovery from wrong first passes; and test on established fine-detail and long-input evaluation sets beyond your own, scoping claims to the domains covered.

## Differentiation within parent

- **Reward the Evidence Stage Separately** — that sibling forces a verbal observation or evidence segment before reasoning and scores its fidelity or coverage; here the intermediate is a spatial or temporal *pointer* into the input (a region, span, candidate or identity) and it is credited by whether re-reading that part enables the answer.
- **Checkable Intermediate Artifacts as Rewards** — that sibling rewards any machine-checkable intermediate (restatements, assertions, numeric anchors, entity values) alongside the answer; here the intermediate is specifically "where to look", often paired with an actual re-acquisition of the input at higher fidelity, and the credit is about sufficiency and efficiency of looking rather than correctness of a stated fact.
- **Process-Level Reasoning Reward Shaping** — that sibling governs the reasoning trace itself (length budget, quality, conformity to reference steps, whether to reason at all); here the governed decision is perceptual acquisition — when to zoom, how many looks, which candidate to keep — not the shape of the verbal reasoning.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when the evidence for an answer occupies a small part of a large or long input, processing the whole input at full fidelity is unaffordable or lossy, and you can define a cheap check of whether a proposed region or span actually carries the evidence. If you cannot score localization without dense annotated boxes, if a single pass already sees the evidence, or if your locate step is a variant of an existing crop-and-zoom policy with no new credit signal, the contribution will read as incremental engineering.

## Tactical failure mode

The dominant failure is the incremental recombination: coarse-to-fine re-inspection, tool-based narrowing, candidate retrieval plus a reasoner, or dense distance rewards stacked on an existing policy-gradient grounding recipe, without a new principle for what makes localization good, judged a near-duplicate of prior iterative-refinement work. The second is the unjustified reward: an unweighted sum of overlap and answer terms that requires annotated boxes, with no component or sensitivity analysis. The third is entangled or unfair attribution: gains that come from doubled test-time search, a strong frozen segmenter, a candidate generator baselines do not get, or a strongly pretrained initialization, so the locate subgoal's contribution is never isolated. The fourth is an unvalidated iterative claim — the policy mostly looks once, the located regions are never checked for containing the evidence, a wrong first pass has no recovery, and overhead is unreported. A candidate can self-check: does my credit signal measure whether the located part carries the evidence, have I beaten the closest crop/zoom or candidate method under matched conditions, and does the model actually use the looking behavior I claim at an honest cost.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Crediting a proposed region by whether a frozen verifier can answer from that region alone, plus a term favoring small regions, teaches a policy to infer where the evidence lies from context even when the target is unreadable at low fidelity.
- Feeding the fine stage's localization quality back as the reward for the coarse proposal stage couples a two-stage locate-then-refine system without separate region labels for the coarse stage.
- Reformulating dense coordinate prediction as choosing among persistent identity marks rendered on the input turns an alignment-hard regression into a compact verifiable selection.
- Training only on instances whose correctness depends on the fidelity of the look, and rewarding correct answers that use fewer escalations, teaches the system to escalate only when the cheap view is insufficient.
- A dense distance-based localization reward in place of a sparse hit reward, with very few curated examples, can match much larger systems trained on far more data.
- Replacing generated position numbers with structural tokens aligned to the input by a contrastive objective lets boundaries be read off from similarity, especially helping multi-part localization.

### Reject lessons
- Adding coarse-to-fine re-inspection or tool-based narrowing to an existing policy-gradient grounding recipe, with only fine-grained design differences from prior iterative refinement, is judged incremental.
- A hierarchical reward that sums an overlap term against annotated regions with an answer-gated term, unweighted and without stability or component analysis, is read as unjustified and inapplicable where region annotations are absent.
- When most of the gain comes from a test-time search that roughly doubles latency, entangled with the training gains, data-efficiency and superiority claims cannot be isolated.
- A learned iterative narrowing policy that in practice calls its tool about once per instance undercuts its own iterative claim and reads as engineering rather than a new paradigm.
- Pairing a reasoner with a strong segmentation or retrieval back end while baselines lack the same back end leaves the comparison unfair and the novelty overstated.
