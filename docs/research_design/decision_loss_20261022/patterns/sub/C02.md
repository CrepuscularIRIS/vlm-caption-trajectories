# C02 — Isolate Weak Sub-Skill, Supervise Intermediates
_parent: **Diagnose the Bottleneck, Then Target It** (`diagnose_then_target`)_ · secondary: Make Intermediate Steps Explicit and Score Them · n=18 (oral 2, spotlight 0, poster 12, reject 4)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to show, with a controlled substitution or isolation test, that a failing system is limited by one low-level sub-skill rather than by the high-level reasoning it is usually blamed for, and then to expand that sub-skill into an explicit intermediate representation that is trained before or alongside the downstream reasoning. The diagnostics are oracle-style: replace raw inputs with ideal structured descriptions and watch the task become solvable; present the same content in two equivalent forms with a simple-to-hard split to expose that only one form generalizes; strip entangling language and commonsense with minimal diagnostic tasks; measure how few generated tokens the sub-skill receives; or decode the relevant variables from internal activations to separate encoded competence from usable performance. The remedy turns the sub-skill into a supervised intermediate whose ground truth is cheap and correct by construction — programmatically generated descriptions, simulator-rendered intermediate fields, a training-only structured view of the input, symbolic unit tokens and decomposed sub-estimates, a task-conditioned structured graph rewarded for alignment, human-derived reasoning patterns, or verifiable pretext tasks that exercise the deficient skill — and training is staged so the weak stage is fixed first and reasoning is optimized after. A related accepted strand reports the diagnosis itself as the contribution: carefully controlled transfer ladders or matrices showing that targeted tuning does not generalize beyond the trained condition.

## Step-by-Step

1. Test the conventional explanation directly with an oracle substitution or controlled isolation. Grant the system perfect intermediate input (an ideal structured description, an equivalent representation in its stronger form) or remove entangling factors with minimal tasks, and show the failure moves with the sub-skill, not with reasoning. Report residual weakness honestly: reviewers questioned bottleneck claims when systems stayed weak even with the oracle, so state what fraction of the gap the substitution explains.
2. Name the specific sub-skill and design an explicit intermediate representation for it whose ground truth comes from construction, not from the system under test. Use programmatic generators, simulators, training-only structured views, or rule-based engines so labels are correct by construction; where an external generator writes intermediates, add human review or verification. Concerns about teacher-generated annotations hallucinating or rationalizing a known answer recur when this is skipped.
3. Train the weak stage first and the reasoning stage second, and make the intermediate a genuine dependency. Supervise the intermediate (or reward its alignment with an annotated structure), then optimize the downstream task conditioned on it; show the final output actually uses the intermediate. Compare against matched alternatives that supply the same kind of signal (another explicit cue of the same type, direct fine-tuning on the target, a plain reasoning-format baseline) so the gain is attributable to this intermediate rather than to any extra signal.
4. Evaluate transfer outside the generator's distribution with fine-tuned baselines, not zero-shot ones. Use external evaluation suites, real instances alongside synthetic ones, and graded shifts (more entities, new appearance, real scenes, sibling tasks); train comparison systems on the same data. Rejects and borderline accepts were repeatedly faulted for training on a self-built synthetic evaluation set and comparing to zero-shot frontier systems, and for gains that do not survive real or standard evaluation suites.
5. Draw a claim that is actionable and scoped to what the diagnosis covered. Tie the lesson to a method or a decision a practitioner can use, and bound it to the tasks, model family and scale tested. Negative or lesson-style results were accepted when cleanly controlled and probed, but rejected when the takeaway was unclear, the setting a single contrived environment, or the lessons already community consensus with no principled method beyond more data.

## Differentiation within parent

- vs **Diagnose Failures, Then Target Training**: that sibling runs ablations or failure diagnostics on a fixed base and routes findings into targeted data, curricula or negatives — the output is a better training mix. This card's diagnostic is specifically an oracle substitution or isolation that pins the failure to one low-level sub-skill, and its remedy is structural: the sub-skill is expanded into an explicit intermediate representation (often generator- or simulator-supplied) that is supervised as its own stage before or alongside the downstream reasoning.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when a system fails tasks that humans find easy, the community attributes the failure to reasoning, and you can build an oracle that hands the system the right intermediate and makes the task solvable. It fits best when a programmatic generator, simulator or training-only structured view can supply correct-by-construction intermediates at scale. If the oracle substitution does not close most of the gap, or the intermediate can only be produced by another model's unverified outputs, the bottleneck claim will not hold and the work will read as a data pipeline.

## Tactical failure mode

Attempts fail most often on ecological scope: the sub-skill is isolated and trained on a self-built synthetic generator, evaluated on that same evaluation set against zero-shot baselines, and never shown to transfer to real instances or standard evaluation suites. A second recurring shape is a diagnosis without an actionable remedy — a controlled study whose conclusion (the sub-skill is weak, targeted data helps, tuning does not generalize) is already consensus or offers no method beyond more data. A third is attribution weakness: the intermediate's gain is not compared with matched alternatives that provide the same kind of signal, so any explicit cue could explain the improvement. A fourth is incremental assembly — adding a missing input channel or chaining standard supervised and reward stages with an existing reward — which reviewers read as obvious. Self-check: does an oracle substitution show the sub-skill explains most of the gap; is the intermediate's ground truth independent of the system and correct by construction; and does the targeted gain transfer beyond the generator against fine-tuned, matched baselines.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Inserting a task-conditioned structured intermediate between reading and planning, generating it first and rewarding its alignment with annotated structure rather than only the final plan, curbs hallucinated plans and reached the top tier with real-world deployment evidence.
- Replacing generic stepwise reasoning with a small set of explicit expert-derived thinking patterns, internalized first and then rewarded during exploration, transfers to unseen variants where surface-artifact memorization fails — paired with a hierarchical out-of-distribution evaluation.
- Rewriting inputs as ideal structured descriptions and finding the task becomes solvable relocates the bottleneck from reasoning to fine-grained reading, justifying a generator that emits tasks with ground-truth descriptions and staged training.
- Presenting identical content in two equivalent forms with a simple-to-hard split isolates a representation imbalance; supervising conversion into the stronger form before reasoning closes the gap, and the conversion can later become implicit.
- A simulator's clean intermediate field — predicted first, then conditioned on — distills low-level dynamics even when the simulator is imperfect in detail, provided matched alternative cues are ruled out.
- A training-only structured view of the input can serve as privileged material for a stronger reasoner to write traces that are transferred to the raw-input system and then refined with rewards.

### Reject lessons
- A controlled synthetic study whose lessons (curricula help, targeted data helps) were already consensus, resting on two near-saturated subtasks and one model family with no principled method beyond data, was judged insufficiently novel.
- A clean matched-budget comparison of two training paradigms in one contrived environment, with both saturating in-task and no actionable takeaway, was rejected as too narrow even after added experiments.
- Training on a self-built synthetic evaluation set and comparing against zero-shot frontier systems, with no real data or standard evaluation suites, leaves generalization undemonstrated.
- Adding a missing input channel to an existing pipeline and retraining with a prior reward, on a small dataset without variance, reads as obvious and incremental.

_evidence note: only 4 rejects (one of which is a later-accepted resubmission of the same study), so reject lessons rest on thin evidence; the two orals use structured intermediates and expert reasoning patterns rather than an explicit oracle-substitution diagnostic._
