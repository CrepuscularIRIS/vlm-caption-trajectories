# C00 — Label-Free Preference Pair Construction
_parent: **Manufacture Contrasts by Controlled Corruption** (`manufacture_contrastive_pairs`)_ · secondary: Substitute Cheap Verifiable Signals for Expensive Supervision · n=46 (oral 0, spotlight 0, poster 35, reject 11)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to manufacture preferred/rejected output pairs without human preference labels, and then to concentrate the contrastive learning signal on exactly the part of the pair that differs. Pairs are produced by one of a small number of recurring generators: the system's own outputs under a clean versus deliberately degraded input (masked, noised, mixed, structurally shuffled, distractor-padded, or a degraded view of the sub-region it attended to); the system's own outputs under a careful versus misleading instruction; unit-level substitution in which one semantic unit of a good output is swapped for a lower-ranked resampled alternative so the quality gap is controlled; ranking of sampled outputs by deterministic task error against ground truth; or ranking by a cheap self-contained signal (internal uncertainty on content-bearing elements, attention allocation to distractors, a component of the system reused as a per-element verifier, a lightweight external similarity term that calibrates a biased self-score). The second half of the move is credit localization: span-, segment- or token-level rewards on the differing content with per-token regularization, an input-side contrast in which the same output must be more likely under the intact input than under the corrupted one, symmetric pairs so the objective's normalizers cancel, listwise ranking over many graded negatives, or per-pair weighting by margin or difficulty. The load-bearing artifact is the pair generator plus a demonstration that the contrast actually depends on the conditioning input rather than on output-only shortcuts.

## Step-by-Step

1. Diagnose the specific way the current contrastive objective fails before building any pairs. Show, with a representation inspection, a gradient argument, a derivation check or a per-element versus whole-output correlation, that the standard pair either ignores the conditioning input, dilutes credit across long shared spans, or carries an uncontrolled quality gap. Accepted papers lead with this mechanism (an objective that never pulls the input toward its correct output, a normalizer that fails to cancel when the conditioning input is swapped, coarse scores that barely track errors); rejected papers asserted a cause ("missing alignment is why it fails") without ruling out alternatives such as training data or instruction tuning.
2. Choose a pair generator whose corruption mirrors a real failure and whose quality gap you control. Prefer generators that are on-policy and input-conditioned — the system's own output under an enhanced versus degraded view, a unit-level substitution with a known error strength, a ratio-controlled mixing that sets the margin — over off-policy text edits or a stronger generator's output treated as preferred. Make the degradation remove semantically load-bearing content (not trivial low-level noise the system can shortcut on), and verify that perturbed inputs actually change the output; capability-gap labels between related generators, where both outputs may be wrong, were judged too noisy to support the claim.
3. Keep the pair's judge independent of what it judges, or show it is not circular. If the ranking signal comes from the system's own internals (uncertainty, attention, self-likelihood), calibrate it with a lightweight external term or validate that the signal ranks labeled candidates correctly; if it comes from a stronger judge or metric, do not use the same judge as the evaluation metric, and ablate whether a weaker judge gives the same gain. Reviewers repeatedly flagged confidently-wrong self-signals, judge metrics reused for training and evaluation, and gains that may be distillation from a larger labeler.
4. Localize credit to the difference and make the objective consistent with its derivation. Put the reward on the spans, segments or tokens that differ, add a contrast on the input side so the preferred output must depend on the intact input, condition both sides on an identical context or use symmetric pairs so normalizing terms cancel, and use listwise or graded multi-negative ranking when one negative under-specifies the failure. Isolate each added term in ablation and against the closest existing variant; rejected papers stacked a symmetric objective, a refreshed reference and a curriculum that each already existed, and could not show which piece was new.
5. Evaluate beyond the failure the pairs target and separate method from data. Report general-capability and helpfulness results alongside the targeted error metric, check output length (shorter answers can masquerade as fewer errors), test more than one base-system family including a current one, train baselines on the same constructed data, and report generation and compute cost of the pair pipeline. Narrow evaluation on one dated backbone with only the targeted metric was the single most common reason rejects stayed rejected even when ratings were mixed-positive.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when the target failure can be induced automatically and cheaply on the same instance — by degrading the input, misleading the instruction, or substituting one unit of a good output — so that each pair differs in a controllable, input-dependent way, and when you can name why the existing whole-output contrast mis-assigns credit. If you cannot produce a negative whose gap you control, or your only ranking signal is the system judging itself with no external check, the contribution collapses into "apply an existing preference objective to new data".

## Tactical failure mode

Papers attempting this tactic fail most often on novelty-by-recombination: the pair generator or objective is a direct application of an existing listwise, symmetric, curriculum or reference-refresh method to a new input setting, and the authors do not isolate what is new against the closest prior variant. A second recurring shape is judge circularity — preference labels come from a stronger model, a capability-gap proxy, or the system's own internal signal, and the same signal (or a sibling of it) is then used for evaluation, so gains may be distillation or reward hacking rather than better alignment. A third is narrow evaluation: a single older backbone, only the targeted error metric, no length or general-capability check, private or single-source data. Finally, adding machinery (interactive evidence retrieval for a judge, multi-round reference resets with re-distillation) without an equal-budget control or downstream validation reads as engineering. Self-check: does the diagnosis show why the standard contrast fails; is the pair's judge independent of the evaluation; and does the gain survive on another base system and on general tasks.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- When preferred and rejected outputs share most of their content, putting the reward only on the differing spans with per-token regularization both sharpens credit and bounds the gradient, which reviewers credited as a principled rather than cosmetic change.
- Contrasting the system's own outputs under an enhanced versus degraded view of the sub-region it already attended to targets processing failures (the evidence was held but misused) rather than missing knowledge, and makes the preference signal depend on the input itself.
- Substituting one semantic unit of a good output with a lower-ranked self-resampled alternative turns the quality gap into a designed quantity, which enables an easy-to-hard ordering of negatives by injected error strength.
- When swapping the conditioning input between the two sides of a pair silently breaks the objective's normalizer cancellation, placing both inputs in one shared context with an explicit selector restores the derivation; a sound theoretical fix of this kind carries a paper even with modest gains.
- Padding existing single-input instances with distractors creates the harder regime for free, and the system's own allocation of attention to the distractors serves as an annotation-free error detector for selecting rejected outputs.
- Calibrating a self-assigned reward with a lightweight external grounding term breaks the self-reinforcement of a dominant-source bias without requiring a stronger judge or human labels.

### Reject lessons
- Applying an existing listwise or symmetric preference method with a new reward source, without a comparison to an on-policy optimizer using the same reward, was read as a direct application with limited novelty.
- Claiming that missing alignment is the main cause of a capability gap, without ruling out pre-training data or instruction tuning as alternatives, left the motivating claim unsubstantiated.
- Using the same automatic judge metric to build preference pairs and to evaluate the result invited reward hacking through longer outputs, and expert raters scored the top-metric method lowest.
- Labeling the stronger of two related generators as always preferred produced noisy pairs where both outputs could be wrong, with no tie statistics or human validation, and the central assumption was judged weak.
- Evaluating only on dated backbones, binary existence-style checks or private data, with gains that shrink on current systems, kept otherwise well-motivated corruption-based methods below the bar.
