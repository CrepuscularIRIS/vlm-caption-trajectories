# C03 — Dedicated Tokens Carrying Intermediate Evidence
_parent: **Add a Dedicated Carrier for Intermediate State** (`dedicated_state_carrier`)_ · secondary: Align Internal Representations to a Teacher · n=26 (oral 1, spotlight 0, poster 18, reject 7)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to stop letting free-form generated text be the only vehicle for intermediate computation, and instead insert a dedicated, learned carrier into the generation stream whose content is tied back to the input. Authors add a control token or action that re-retrieves, points to, or copies stored input features on demand; latent positions whose hidden states are trained to reconstruct the relevant input embeddings or to align with a frozen specialist encoder's features; compact tokens into which a whole reasoning stage, a long rationale, or an auxiliary signal is compressed (often with a decoder that reconstructs text to audit what survived); generated intermediate renderings or executable edits that re-expose the input; or an internal structured interface (an induced graph over variable tokens, an explicit localize-or-abstain marker) that replaces prompt-level hints. Supervision for the carrier is manufactured — localization or step-to-input alignment labels from automated pipelines, a stronger generator's traces, self-generated filtered traces — and training usually runs as a staged curriculum, sometimes ending in a policy-gradient stage adapted to replay latent steps. The strongest variant begins from a diagnostic: grounding measurably decays as the generated context grows, or an existing intermediate is shown to act as a compact representation, and the carrier is then designed to restore or distill exactly that.

## Step-by-Step

1. Establish, with a direct measurement, what information the free-form intermediate loses or what it secretly carries. Show reliance on the input decaying as generated context lengthens (degrade the input while varying preceding context, or track attention to input positions), or show through attention and embedding analysis that an intermediate behaves as a compact domain-aligned representation. Rejects that asserted the decay without showing it causes the performance loss, or whose pointing variant barely beat a plain coordinate variant, were faulted for an unproven causal link.
2. Design the carrier to reuse what the system already has before generating anything new. Prefer re-selecting stored input features, reconstructing the system's own input embeddings, or aligning to a frozen specialist's features over adding external converters, detectors or generation heads; state why the carrier lives in the same space as the reasoning. Dependence on an external detector or converter trained on narrow data was a decisive rejection reason, and an added pathway must be shown not to erode existing capabilities.
3. Give the carrier its own supervision target and isolate it from the data that comes with it. Build alignment, reconstruction or localization targets automatically, then run a data-only control — the same traces trained without the carrier, text-only fine-tuning plus the policy stage on the same data, or simply repeating initial features at every step — so gains cannot be attributed to a stronger teacher, longer traces or a newer backbone. The most frequent concern across both posters and rejects is that the constructed dataset, not the carrier, explains the improvement.
4. Make the carrier inspectable and account for its cost. Decode or visualize what the carrier holds (a text reconstruction with a reference baseline, rendered latents, replay success and invalid-address rates, the selected positions), report latency, memory and compute against an equal-budget baseline, and analyze what happens when the carrier is wrong. Opaque latents, unreported overhead and unexamined error propagation are repeatedly cited even on accepted papers.
5. Evaluate beyond the regime the carrier data came from and state the novelty relative to the crowded neighborhood. Test on more than one backbone and on out-of-domain or non-synthetic tasks, include the closest prior interleaved, latent and pointer-style methods as baselines, and articulate what the carrier does that they do not. Evaluations confined to one synthetic environment, one task family or one backbone, with marginal gains and missing nearest-neighbor comparisons, are the recurring reject profile.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when you can show that intermediate information is lost or poorly carried by free-form output — grounding fading over long generations, verbose stages that are mostly redundant, an auxiliary signal the main system cannot consume directly — and you can build a target for a dedicated carrier from material the system already exposes or from an automated labeling pipeline. It is strongest when a measured decay or a measured compression effect motivates the carrier. If you cannot separate the carrier's effect from the new training data, or if the carrier only works with an external specialist tool, the result will read as data distillation or tool engineering rather than a new mechanism.

## Tactical failure mode

Attempts fail most often on novelty in a crowded space: latent reasoning, interleaved re-looking, pointer heads and sketch-style scratchpads already exist, and a carrier that is a straightforward port of one of them, without head-to-head comparison, is judged incremental. A second recurring shape is confounded evidence — gains that may come from a large-teacher dataset, longer traces or a newer backbone, with no data-only or repeat-the-input-features control. A third is a narrow, often synthetic evaluation (one environment, one task family, one backbone) with marginal or uneven gains and unreported inference overhead, which makes the carrier look like an interpretability device rather than a capability improvement. A fourth is reliance on an external converter or detector that limits generality and raises the question of whether the intermediate step is necessary at all. Self-check: is the information loss or compression effect directly measured; is the carrier's gain separated from its training data; and has it been tested outside its source regime against the nearest carrier-style baselines with cost reported.

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Treating a costly reasoner's intermediate output as a compact, domain-aligned representation — demonstrated through attention and embedding analysis — lets that representation be distilled into a lightweight model, and the mechanistic account is what lifts the work to the top tier.
- Inserting learned positions before every step and aligning them to a frozen specialist encoder's dense features gives each step a refreshed grounding checkpoint; showing that baselines degrade as reasoning lengthens while the method keeps improving is the persuasive evidence.
- Retrieving already-computed, high-resolution features by model-predicted address — instead of re-encoding sub-regions — improves detail-sensitive tasks while compressing the default input, and fair baselines plus efficiency analysis carry acceptance.
- A controlled experiment that degrades the input while varying preceding generated context can quantify how sequential coherence erodes reliance on evidence, justifying decomposition into independently grounded units.
- Replacing prompt-supplied structural hints with an internal structured interface (an induced graph over variable tokens) is credited when prompt-level injection is shown to be ineffective or harmful.
- Training a reconstruction target on generated latent states so they reproduce the relevant input embeddings turns intermediate reasoning into input-space computation; an adapted policy stage that replays latent steps can follow.

### Reject lessons
- Compressing reasoning stages into latent tokens in a way that closely resembles existing latent-reasoning methods, compared only against the uncompressed base and without latency or FLOP figures, reads as limited novelty with weak evidence.
- An internal scratchpad evaluated on a single synthetic environment, with gains often under a point, is judged an interpretability tool rather than a reasoning improvement.
- Binding entities to regions through an external detector trained on synthetic data limits generalization, and reviewers held that dependence decisive even after rebuttal.
- Routing a structured input through an executable symbolic intermediate whose necessity is not shown — the system could take the input directly, and the gain over the base is small — leaves the bridge unjustified, especially when traces are filtered only by final-answer correctness.
- Diagnosing attention decay but not showing that the decay causes the error, while evaluating on a single task family and backbone, leaves the pointer mechanism's contribution borderline.
