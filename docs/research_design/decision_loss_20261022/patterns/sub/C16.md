# C16 — Restore Signal Under Sparse Rewards
_parent: **Repair a Degenerate Learning Signal** (`repair_degenerate_signal`)_ · secondary: none · n=57 (oral 1, spotlight 2, poster 44, reject 10)

## Tactical pattern

The shared move is to treat a stalled policy-gradient run not as ordinary sparsity or convergence but as a measurable pathology of the group-relative update itself, name its cause from group-level statistics, and insert one targeted repair at the point where the signal dies. The pathologies are recurring and specific: sampled groups whose outcomes are all-success or all-failure so normalized advantages are exactly zero; near-uniform groups whose tiny scale statistic inflates their advantages; a shrinking share of rollouts that carry any gradient as training proceeds; several reward dimensions or heterogeneous sub-populations whose advantages cancel or are dominated by the majority; rollouts truncated at a horizon cap that are silently scored as failures; and newly added actions or modes that fail early, get punished and are abandoned. The repairs fall into a small set of levers: re-form the comparison group (difficulty-matched buckets, per-dimension or per-partition advantages, input variants pooled into a shared baseline); inject a reference signal into the group (an annotation-derived solution, an expert trajectory used as a calibration baseline, a reward-gated imitation term, a teacher's dense per-step divergence); restore within-group variance at the data source (difficulty-adapted, answer-preserving input edits or perturbations; resampling until enough non-zero groups exist; replaying past high-advantage samples); add an intrinsic or densified signal when no task reward exists yet (cross-modal or learned-predictor prediction error, kernel-smoothed sparse outcomes, rollout-consensus pseudo-labels); or rescale/mask advantages (by rarity, domain imbalance, mode frequency, truncation status). The headline evidence is that the diagnosed statistic (fraction of zero-advantage groups, reward variance, effective batch, mode usage, rollout diversity) actually moves under the fix and that this movement, not extra compute, explains the gain.

## Step-by-Step

1. Diagnose the degeneracy from group-level training statistics before touching the reward. Log the fraction of groups with zero advantage, the within-group reward variance, the effective number of gradient-carrying samples, per-dimension advantage signs, or usage rates of each action/mode over training, and show the specific collapse curve. The strongest accepted work names a pathology that ordinary sparsity or convergence would hide (a normalization artifact in low-variance groups, a silent stall that looks like convergence, truncation scored as failure, a new action punished before it can succeed). A premise that is asserted rather than shown — e.g. that extreme groups "matter" with no measurement — is a direct rejection cause.
2. Attribute the collapse to one cause and choose the lever that targets that cause. If groups are uniform because instances are too easy or too hard, regenerate or perturb inputs to the learnability frontier under an answer-preserving constraint; if the policy cannot reach any success, inject a reference solution, expert calibration or gated imitation; if signals cancel, separate the advantage computation by dimension, partition, domain or mode; if no task reward exists, add an intrinsic signal tied to what the agent fails to predict. Keep the fix at the stage where the signal dies (sampling, grouping, normalization, or update) and leave the rest of the objective intact so the attribution stays clean.
3. Make the injected or reshaped signal safe for the on-policy update. Rescale, anchor or asymmetrically gate any off-policy or external sample so it guides without dominating; mask rather than penalize truncated rollouts; reset or slow-update any learned predictor that would otherwise drain the intrinsic signal; temper normalization so artificially non-zero advantages in fully collapsed groups do not reinforce mistakes. Rejected designs pooled external responses without importance correction or with top-k selection that could let outside samples crowd out the policy's own, or leaned on an unverified model judge to localize errors.
4. Prove the gain comes from the repair, not from more budget or more supervision. Match rollout counts, wall-clock and teacher/judge cost against the baseline; compare against the simplest alternatives a reviewer will name (drop the scale term, enlarge the group, plain filtering of uniform groups, entropy regularization, more demonstrations, resampling-free reweighting) and against the current strong group-relative variants; and show the diagnosed statistic itself recovering (reward variance, valid-advantage share, mode balance, diversity). Unmatched sampling budgets, missing compute ledgers and fixes that do not clearly beat a simpler baseline are the most frequent concerns, and a novelty doubt combined with missing simple baselines sank several rejects.
5. Bound the claim and show the repair travels. Demonstrate the fix across more than one backbone, domain or task family, report seeds or variance where gains are a few points, and run sensitivity on every new threshold, gate or weight. State the precondition honestly (most of these fixes need a base policy that sometimes succeeds or a reference signal that is reliable), and avoid overstated framings such as "self-evolving" or "first" when an external judge, teacher or generator supplies the signal — reviewers repeatedly downgraded such wording, and rejects in this cluster were typically a transferred recipe from another domain with no new diagnosis.

## When to pick this one

Reach for this when an outcome-reward policy-gradient run stalls or underperforms and you can show, from logged group statistics, that the update is starved for a concrete reason (uniform groups, cancelling dimensions, majority-dominated advantages, truncation, early punishment of a new action) and you have a cheap lever that acts exactly there. If the base policy essentially never succeeds and you have no reliable reference or intrinsic signal, or if you cannot separate your fix from extra rollouts or a stronger teacher, this tactic reduces to "spend more compute" and will be read as incremental.

## Tactical failure mode

Papers in this cluster fail most often by porting a known stabilization recipe (dynamic sampling, leave-one-out baselines, replay, curriculum filtering, reweighting) into a new setting without a new diagnosis — reviewers call it engineering adaptation, and without a rebuttal or with low scores this is decisive. A second recurring shape is an unsupported premise: the paper asserts that a statistic causes the stall but never shows the statistic moving, or the proposed difficulty or diversity proxy is never validated as measuring what it claims. A third is confounded attribution: the method samples more rollouts, uses a stronger teacher or judge, or draws on extra demonstrations, so the gain cannot be credited to the repair. A fourth is unverified external signal — a model judge locating errors, an intrinsic reward that may reward superficial novelty, a curiosity signal with no downstream task evidence — whose reliability is assumed rather than measured. A candidate can self-check: is the pathology shown in the training statistics, does the fix move that statistic, and does it beat the simplest named alternative at matched budget?

## Examples
### Accepted lessons
- Scaling the group-normalized advantage by the rarity of each sample's sub-population and its within-population difficulty moves imbalance correction from the data loader into the critic-free objective, and lets one policy perform evenly over skewed heterogeneous data.
- A training stall that looks like convergence can be shown to be advantage collapse on uniform-outcome prompts; replaying past high-advantage samples restores the effective batch and outperforms simply filtering the uniform groups.
- Using the gap between the agent's predicted next state and the actual observation as both a grounding loss and an intrinsic reward gives exploration a target before any task reward exists — provided the alignment head is periodically re-initialized so the signal does not drain.
- When rewards are discrete and groups near-constant, adding reward anchors to each group and tempering normalization by group variance yields a principled non-zero signal; ablations over group size and anchor settings make the fix credible.
- Rollouts that hit the step cap should have their advantages masked rather than scored as failures; otherwise the policy is biased toward short trajectories and cannot extend its interaction depth at inference.
- Injecting a reference solution or expert trajectory into the group works when it is reshaped — downscaled, anchored, or used only as a calibration baseline gated by correctness and rarity — so it guides the update without dominating on-policy samples.

### Reject lessons
- Importing a stabilization recipe from another domain (dynamic sampling of non-zero-advantage groups, leave-one-out baselines) without a new diagnosis, and validating only in simulation on two models, reads as engineering adaptation.
- Pooling stronger external responses with policy rollouts and keeping the reward-ranked top subset, without importance correction or comparison to prior off-policy-guided methods, is judged overlapping and unsafe (selected groups may be entirely external).
- A regrouping or reweighting fix whose difficulty proxies are unvalidated, whose motivating premise is not measured, and which does not clearly beat simpler baselines (dropping the scale term, more rollouts, prior perturbation methods) is turned away as unjustified complexity.
- Dense rewards from a model judge that locates mistaken steps against a reference trajectory are rejected when the judge's reliability on cascading errors is unverified and the exploration claim is not controlled for sample count.
- Task-free exploration with intrinsic rewards that never demonstrates downstream task competence, and whose diversity metrics are tied to the training reward, cannot show it is more than superficial novelty.
